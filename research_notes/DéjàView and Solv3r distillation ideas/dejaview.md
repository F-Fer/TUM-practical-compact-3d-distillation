## 1. Architecture: what is looped, what state is carried, conditioning, heads, intermediate decoding

### Takeaway
DéjàView is a DINOv2 ViT-B/14 encoder (86.6M of its 117.1M parameters, trained with an LR multiplier of 0.1, not frozen), followed by **one** shared "AA" block pair (frame attention, then global attention; 14.5M parameters) applied K times, and finally three small heads (rays, depth, camera). The only state carried between iterations is the token tensor z_k. Nothing is injected into the loop: no re-injection of z_0 and no predictions (cameras, depth) fed back. Each step is conditioned on a continuous time interval (t_now, t_next) through a zero-initialised MLP that produces three channel-wise gates. Any intermediate z_k can be decoded (the decoders are pre-norm), but the model was trained with supervision only at the final step.

### Cited Findings
- Pipeline: DINOv2 patch tokens, plus R=4 register tokens and a camera token per view (two camera-token parameter sets, one for the reference view and one shared by all other views), with 2D RoPE on patch positions; then "a single looped transformer block with frame-wise and global attention sub-blocks … applied recurrently … for K steps"; "Two heads decode the final tokens into per-view depth and ray predictions" [read] — [paper §3.3, Fig. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: `model/dvlt/model.py` L610-629 (`forward_train` / `forward_inference`: `x = cat([camera_token, register_token, z_0])`, then `_solve_*`, then `_decode`).
- The looped unit is `LoopedAABlock` = `frame_attn` (a `DepthScaledAttentionBlock` with RoPE, over the P tokens of each frame) followed by `global_attn` (the same block type without RoPE, over all S·P tokens of the scene) [read] — code: `model/dvlt/blocks.py` (class `LoopedAABlock`). With the released config there is exactly one such block, `recurrent_blocks[0]`, reused at every step (`_interval_step` always uses `self.recurrent_blocks[0]`, model.py L406-421) [read].
- Update rule (paper Eq. 2, identical in code): z' = z_k + s_attn ⊙ LS1(Attn(LN1(z_k))); z'' = z' + s_mlp ⊙ LS2(MLP(LN2(z'))); z_{k+1} = s_out ⊙ z''. Here s = 1 + MLP(γ(t_k, t_{k+1})), with γ the concatenated sinusoidal embeddings of t_now and t_next and the last layer zero-initialised, so the gates start at identity [read] — [paper §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: `blocks.py` `DepthScaledAttentionBlock.forward`, `depth_scaling.py` `IntervalDepthScaling` (hidden 64, `nn.init.zeros_` on the last layer). Each of the two sub-blocks has its own gate MLP.
- No input injection: z_0 enters only as the initial state, and the loop sees nothing but x [read] — code: model.py L464-478 and L542-550 (the loop body is only `x = self._interval_step(x, t_now, t_next, …)`). Solv3R confirms independently that DéjàView lacks input injection and lists adding it as one of its changes — [Solv3R Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- Step grid in the code: `ts = torch.linspace(0, 1, K)`, step i uses (ts[i], ts[i+1]), and the last step uses (1.0, 1.0), a "terminal-step sentinel (delta_t = 0)". K=1 therefore runs a single (0, 1) step, and for K≥2 the first K−1 steps cover [0, 1] with dt = 1/(K−1), followed by one zero-width step [read] — code: model.py L464-478, L542-550. The paper describes the grid differently, as t_k = k/K over K intervals [read] — [paper §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). The code is what the released weights ran with.
- Heads: the ray decoder and the depth decoder are each `proj_in` (768→384) followed by 2 pre-norm transformer blocks (dim 384, 6 heads, RoPE, no LayerScale in the released config) and an output stage. Rays use a linear pixel-shuffle head with 6 channels (direction + origin). Depth uses a MoGe-style progressive ConvTranspose head (dims 256/128/64) with a 2-channel output (depth `exp_clamped` + confidence `exp_plus_one`). World points = R_o + D·R_d [read] — code: `model/dvlt/heads.py` `DecoderHead`, model.py L563-606; [paper §3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). Decoders are per-frame (no cross-frame attention) and are run in `decode_chunk_size` chunks along B·S [read] — model.py L197-202.
- Camera head: a `SimpleCameraHead` MLP on the camera token at the output of the ray-decoder transformer predicts a 9-D (t, quaternion, FoV) encoding. It is trained with `CameraLoss`, but **inference poses come from the rays**: `rays_to_pose` runs a RANSAC homography fit on rays downsampled to the patch grid, inside `torch.no_grad()` [read] — code: `heads.py` `SimpleCameraHead`, model.py L939-968, `common/rays.py` L625-686 (`with torch.no_grad(): … _camray_to_caminfo(…, reproj_threshold=0.2)`); paper: the camera MLP "provides a faster alternative to the rays-derived recovery …, which remains our default at inference" — [paper §3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Intermediate decoding: "Decoding the intermediate state of a single K=16 forward pass at iterations k ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} shows progressively sharper geometry and more accurate camera poses", and per-iteration decoding "yields monotone improvement of pose and pointmap metrics across the trained step range" [read] — [paper Fig. 1, Fig. 3a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). Because each decoder branch is pre-norm, "the LayerNorm at the start of its first transformer block absorbs the component of ∆z_k parallel to z_k that drives the norm growth" [read] — [paper §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Parameter breakdown from the checkpoint header (all fp32, 117.08M in total): `patch_embed_encoder` 86.58M, `recurrent_blocks` 14.50M (per sub-block: attention 2.36M, MLP 4.72M, depth-scale gate MLP 0.158M, LayerScale 768+768), `depth_decoder` 11.40M, `ray_decoder` 4.30M, `camera_head` 0.30M [read from `model.safetensors` header] — local file `/scratch/vc17/pr26rb00/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--nvidia--dvlt/snapshots/20f919875a00ee3ab2e840f2f31580f4652b7c5e/model.safetensors`. The paper reports 117M — [paper Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Emergent behaviour: the global-attention maps "start diffuse and progressively concentrate on the corresponding counterpart of the queried patch", which the authors call an "emergent iterative correspondence search" [read] — [paper supp. C, Fig. 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).

### Inferences
- [inferred] The loop is a learned, time-parameterised flow over a fixed horizon [0, 1]: the gates know where they are and how big the step is. This makes K a property of the *schedule*, not of the weights, and it is also why intermediate states of a K=16 run are not the same as the final states of a K=k run (see §3).
- [inferred] Two thirds of the loop's own parameters sit in the attention and MLP weights. The 0.32M gate parameters are the only parts that see (t_now, t_next), so they are the natural small "schedule adapter" for few-step behaviour (see §6a).
- [inferred] There is no input channel for cameras, depth or any other estimate. Feeding refined cameras back into the loop therefore needs a new, trained injection module (§6c).
- [inferred] The camera head gives a *differentiable* pose readout, while the default ray-based pose fit is not differentiable (RANSAC under no_grad). Any training loss on poses that should back-propagate into the loop must use the camera head, or rays directly (the ray L1 loss already does this).

### Gaps
- The paper does not say whether the camera token is read before or after the decoder LayerNorm. In the code it is read before the final `norm` (`features = x` is taken before `self.norm`), but I did not check whether this matters.
- No per-component FLOP breakdown is given in the paper; the one in §4 is my own estimate.

## 2. Training recipe: K sampling, supervision, losses, data, compute

### Takeaway
K is sampled per batch from Beta(2, 1) scaled to [8, 16], so it is skewed towards 16, and the model has **never been trained at K < 8**. Supervision is applied only to the final state: there is no deep supervision and no teacher or distillation loss. Training uses full back-propagation through the loop with per-call gradient checkpointing, not truncated BPTT. The losses are L2 depth + depth-gradient + L1 rays + L2 pointmap + L1 camera, on DUSt3R-normalised scale. Data: 29 public datasets, 2-18 views per sample at 504 px, 128 H100s, 200K iterations, then a 40K-iteration stage 2 that fine-tunes only the depth decoder.

### Cited Findings
- K sampling: "K ∼ Beta(2, 1) scaled into [8, 16] during training so that a single checkpoint supports any step count in this range", with one K per batch [read] — [paper §4 Implementation, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: model.py L390-404 `_sample_K` (E[K] = min + a/(a+b)·span ≈ 13.3) and `_solve_train_linspace_k`. The checkpoint `config.json` has `min_steps 8, num_steps 16, k_sampler_beta_a 2, k_sampler_beta_b 1, k_sampling linspace, time_conditioning interval, recurrence_mode gated` [read]. K is synchronised across DDP ranks (`sync_stochastic_depth`, seed 42+step) [read] — model.py L442-453.
- `stochastic_depth=0.3` appears in the config but is only used on the `k_sampling=None` paths (`_solve_train_random` / `_prefix`). The released linspace model therefore does not drop steps; it only varies K. `drop_path=0.1` (DropPath on the residual branches inside the loop block) is active in training [read] — model.py L455-462, `blocks.py`.
- Final-step supervision only: "we refine an internal state with a looped block and supervise only at the final step. This avoids running the decoder and computing a loss at every intermediate step, sparing (K−1) decoder forward and backward passes" [read] — [paper §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: `forward_train` decodes `features` once, after `_solve_train` (model.py L610-618).
- Backprop: there is no truncation. Gradient checkpointing is applied to `recurrent_blocks`, `ray_decoder` and `depth_decoder` by default; the wrapper replaces each block's `forward`, so every loop call is checkpointed separately [read] — model.py L349-352; `model/base.py` L449-551; `dvlt-large.yaml` sets `trainer.gradient_checkpointing: True`.
- Losses (paper Eq. 4): ‖ŝD_θ − s̄D‖₂ + L_grad (multi-scale ℓ1 depth gradients, only on synthetic data) + ‖ŝR_θ − s̄R‖₁ + ‖ŝX_θ − s̄X‖₂ + L_cam, where L_cam = ℓ1 on translation, rotation and FoV weighted (1, 1, 0.5). All terms have equal weight, and predictions and GT are each normalised by the inverse mean point norm [read] — [paper §3.5, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: `DVLT.DEFAULT_LOSS_CONFIG` (model.py L643-651: pmap/depth with `disable_conf=True`, ray, cam), `model_components/loss/ray.py` (L1 on 6-channel rays; only the origins are scale-normalised; views with ≤100 valid pixels are masked out).
- Two stages. Stage 1 trains end to end with a linear depth head and plain L2 for 200K iterations at LR 3e-4, AdamW (wd 0.05), cosine schedule without warmup, and a 0.1× LR on DINOv2. Stage 2 swaps in the conv depth head and fine-tunes only the depth decoder for 40K iterations at 1e-4 (500 warmup steps), with confidence-weighted depth loss (λ_c = 0.2) and the ray and camera losses disabled [read] — [paper §3.5, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: `config/experiments/dvlt-large.yaml`, `dvlt-large-depthconv-stage2.yaml` (`finetune_mode: depth_decoder`). Training the conv head with the confidence loss end to end instead is clearly worse (AUC@3 23.0 vs 31.0 in the ablation setting), while stage 2 leaves pose unchanged and raises IR by 0.5 [read] — [paper Tables 7-8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- No teacher: "we train a single shared block end-to-end on a 3D reconstruction task loss, with no teacher network and no distillation targets" (unlike RAPTOR, which distils a pretrained ViT into looped blocks) [read] — [paper §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Scale: "train on 128 H100 GPUs with V ∈ [2, 18] views per scene at 504-pixel longest-edge resolution. Each training step uses up to 4,608 images and a fixed tokens budget (≈2.5M tokens)" [read] — [paper §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). Per GPU (code): `tokens_per_batch: 20736`, `images_per_batch: 36`, `images_per_element: [2, 18]`, comment "16-node launch" [read] — `dvlt-large.yaml`. The ablations (Tables 4-5) are 100K-iteration runs with 2-10 views [read] — `dvlt-large-ablation.yaml`; [paper §4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Data: a mixture of 29 datasets (Aria Synthetic Env. 13.8%, WildRGB-D 9.3%, TRELLIS 9.0%, CO3D 8.8%, Taskonomy 7.3%, ScanNet 6.2%, DL3DV 5.8%, ..., ScanNet++ 3.96%, ...), sampled with temperature α = 0.5 (p_i ∝ √N_i). ScanNet++ uses a scene-level train/eval split [read] — [paper supp. A, Table 6, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); code: `config/experiments/data/mixed_all.yaml` (`repeat_factors`).

### Inferences
- [inferred] Our K sweep collapses below K≈6-8 (`local_measurements.md`), and that is extrapolation in *dt*, not a capacity limit. In code terms, training only ever saw dt ∈ [1/15, 1/7] plus the dt = 0 terminal step; K=6 means dt = 0.2, K=4 dt = 1/3, K=3 dt = 0.5. The paper itself states that quality degrades "when K_inf is pushed substantially outside" [K_min, K_max].
- [inferred] Final-step-only supervision means the intermediate states of a run were never asked to be decodable. The fact that they decode monotonically (Fig. 3a) is emergent, and is what makes a training-free early exit possible at all.
- [inferred] There is no stated GPU-hour budget. A rough guess of the order of magnitude: 200K iterations on 128 H100s is likely several days, i.e. 10⁴ H100-hours or more. That is far beyond our budget, so re-training from scratch is out of the question; only fine-tuning is realistic.

### Gaps
- Wall-clock training time and GPU-hours are not reported, and the H100 memory variant is not stated.
- The paper does not say which loss weights or data mix the 100K-iteration ablations used beyond "the same data".

## 3. The paper's own analysis of K: quality vs K, extrapolation, convergence, early exit, compute

### Takeaway
Within the trained range [8, 16], quality rises monotonically with K. **The paper's headline numbers use K_inf = 16, not 12.** Beyond 16, AUC@3 peaks and then degrades, and the model collapses by K ≈ 30-100 because a few channels grow without bound. The recurrence is not a fixed point: the state norm grows, but its direction converges ("directional refinement"). For a sub-16 budget, running a proper K-step schedule beats early-stopping a K=16 run (AUC@3 0.44 vs 0.31 at K=8). There is no adaptive halting in the paper. Shared weights beat 16 untied blocks.

### Cited Findings
- **Default K mismatch.** The paper: "We run K_inf = 16 steps at inference" [read] — [paper §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). The HF `config.json` has `"inference_steps": 12`, and the `DVLT` class default is `inference_steps=12` (model.py L680), which our adapter uses (`scripts/baselines.py` L173-182) [read]. The repo's Hydra path (`config/experiments/dvlt.yaml` → `config/schema.py` `DVLTModelConfig.inference_steps = None`, L92-145) passes `None`, and `DVLTModel` then falls back to `num_steps = 16` (model.py L261) [read]. That path is used by `scripts/test.py` and the Gradio demo via `util/model_registry.py`. [inferred] The README's reference benchmark (DTU AUC@3 0.8319, ETH3D 0.6604, ...) and the paper tables are therefore at K=16, while our "default" K=12 runs are 4 steps below the paper's operating point. My FLOP estimate in §4 (≈76 TFLOPs at K=16 vs the paper's 75.9) supports this.
- Step-count ablation (100K-iteration models, average over 5 benchmarks): Fixed K=12: AUC@3 30.4, AUC@30 85.5, IR 67.6; variable [8, 16] at K_inf=12: 29.6 / 85.4 / 66.7; Fixed K=16: 33.8 / 86.8 / 69.6; variable at K_inf=16: 33.3 / 86.9 / 69.2. Variable-K training "matches rather than exceeds fixed-K at the same inference budget" [read] — [paper Table 5, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). [inferred] At this scale, going from K=12 to K=16 is worth +3.7 AUC@3 (≈+12% relative).
- Extrapolation: "Pose AUC@3° peaks near the trained budget, then starts to degrade after. Pose AUC@30° and Pointmap Rel. L2 remain stable up to approximately K_inf = 30 but eventually collapse" (sweep to 100 steps). Cause: "some feature channels grow unbounded as we scale beyond K_max". Attempts with looped-transformer stabilisation (Yang et al. 2024) "plateau past the trained budget … at significant additional training cost" [read] — [paper supp. D, Fig. 7, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). Solv3R measures the released DéjàView at AUC@5 76.5 at 16 calls and 0.0 at 128 and 1024 calls [read] — [Solv3R Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- Convergence: "our recurrence does not converge to a fixed point in feature space … the state norm ∥z_k∥ grows monotonically with k", while cos(z_k, z_K) rises to 1 and the relative update ∥∆z_k∥/∥z_k∥ decays "from ∼0.5 at the first step to ∼0.1 at the last" (Fig. 3b: about 5×) [read] — [paper §3.4, Fig. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Below K_max: "a full K_inf-step forward with uniform time interval conditioning calibrated for that budget" vs "early-stopping a K_max-step rollout by reading z_k at k = K_inf": "the full pass strictly dominates early-stopping for every k < K_max, with the largest gap at the smallest budget. At K_inf = 8, Pose AUC@3° rises from 0.31 to 0.44" [read] — [paper supp. E, Fig. 8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). Fig. 8 covers only K ∈ [8, 16].
- Block design (100K-iteration models, average over 5 benchmarks): decoupled 16 blocks: AUC@3 23.0, IR 61.1; shared: 30.2 / 66.4; + residual gates: 31.5 / 67.0; + state gate s_out (full model): 33.3 / 69.2. "weight sharing alone already outperforms the decoupled architecture despite having 16× fewer parameters" [read] — [paper Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- Compute (24 views, A100): DéjàView 117M params, 75.9 TFLOPs (3.2 per image), 4.9 GiB peak, IR 80.3, AUC@30 91.8; DA3-L 356M, 71.4 TFLOPs, 7.3 GiB; VGGT 1257M, 190 TFLOPs, 14.7 GiB; Pi3 959M, 153.8 TFLOPs [read] — [paper Table 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). No latency or K-vs-FLOPs curve is given.
- Adaptive halting: none in DéjàView. The related work mentions Universal Transformers' "adaptive halting" only as prior work [read] — [paper §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).

### Inferences
- [inferred] Per-scene adaptive K is structurally awkward with the released model. Interval conditioning fixes the schedule (dt) before the first step, so a halting rule either commits to K up front, or early-stops a long schedule and pays the Fig. 8 penalty (0.31 vs 0.44 at k=8).
- [inferred] "Directional refinement", where the update norm decays about 5× within [8, 16], suggests that a relative-update stopping signal like Solv3R's could also work as a *difficulty* proxy in the released model. But it has no stationary meaning there, because the update size depends on dt, which the schedule fixes.
- [inferred] On our benchmark (larger S than the paper's 24 views; 100 frames for 7-Scenes and ScanNet++), K=12→16 is likely a cheap gain: about +4 × 0.045 s ≈ +0.2 s per forward on the 5090, extrapolated from our K sweep. The coordinator's probe job already queues K=16, so it should be confirmed there.

### Gaps
- The paper gives no curve below K=8 (Fig. 8 starts at 8). Our in-house sweep is the only source for K<8.
- The values at intermediate K in Fig. 8 can't be read precisely from the text extraction; only the K=8 pair (0.31/0.44) is stated in the text.

## 4. Released code: what is there, how hard fine-tuning is, memory at 16-32 views

### Takeaway
The release includes the full training stack: a Hydra + accelerate trainer, all losses, the stage-1, stage-2 and ablation configs, eval loaders (DTU, ETH3D, 7-Scenes, ScanNet++, nuScenes), and **one** training data loader (ScanNet++, which reads the preprocessed undistorted DSLR data). The other 28 training-set configs reference parsers that are not released. Freezing everything except the loop block, its gates or the heads takes a few lines (`finetune_mode` already covers four variants), and extending K downward is a config change (`min_steps`, Beta parameters). By my estimate, a fine-tuning step with a frozen encoder at 32 views × 504×378 needs about 8-12 GB with the built-in checkpointing, so it fits easily on a 32 GB RTX 5090, and 16 views probably fit on the 10 GB RTX 3080.

### Cited Findings
- README release status: inference code, weights and evaluation code are released; training code is released with only the "ScanNet++ training dataset loader", and "other training dataset loaders" are not yet released [read] — `third_party/dvlt/README.md`. The training command is `python -m dvlt.scripts.train --config-name dvlt-large data=scannetpp` [read] — README.
- Data plumbing: `config/experiments/data/train_datasets/scannetpp.yaml` targets `dvlt.data.datasets.parser.dataverse.DataverseTrainDataset` with `dataverse_cfg.target: scannetpp.ScanNetpp`, reading `${user.data_root}/train/scannetpp/scannetpp_undistort` (`undistorted_images`, `undistorted_depth`, `nerfstudio/transforms_undistorted.json`) and `nvs_sem_train.txt` [read]. The parser resolves `target` under `dvlt.data.sources.datasets` and falls back to `GenericDataset` otherwise [read] — `data/datasets/parser/dataverse.py` L198-240, `data/sources/__init__.py` L13-37. `docs/INSTALL.md` still says the "dataset parsers depend on the `dataverse` package" from an unnamed remote (`<YOUR-DATAVERSE-REMOTE>`), and that "the full training stack does not" work without it [read]. [inferred] ScanNet++ seems self-contained in `dvlt.data.sources.datasets.scannetpp`; the other 28 configs would hit `GenericDataset` and need the private package or our own parsers. `TrainDataset` (`data/datasets/train.py`) provides `prepare_view` (crop/resize/rotate/unproject) and `pick_neighbor_views` (by pose or index), so a new parser only implements `get_data` and a few counts [read].
- ScanNet++ preprocessing for the train and eval layout is documented in `scripts/preprocess/preprocess_scannetpp.md` [read] (file list). The eval sets are DTU, ETH3D, 7-Scenes, ScanNet++ and nuScenes (`data/benchmark.yaml`, `test_datasets/*.yaml`) [read].
- Built-in partial fine-tuning: `finetune_mode ∈ {depth_output, depth_decoder, depth_decoder_recurrent, all_heads}` freezes everything and then unfreezes the chosen modules. Gradient checkpointing is restricted to the trained modules, and `trainer.lr_param_group_multipliers` sets per-group LRs (e.g. `recurrent_blocks: 0.01`) [read] — model.py L707-722, L771-784, L830-925. A "loop block only" or "gates only" mode is not built in, but it amounts to setting `requires_grad` on `recurrent_blocks[0]` or its `depth_scale` submodules and adding one param group [inferred].
- K range and schedule are constructor arguments (`num_steps`, `min_steps`, `k_sampler_beta_a/b`, `inference_steps`), and `inference_steps` "May exceed num_steps" [read] — model.py L118-202. Our branch `dvlt-fewer-steps` only sets `inference_steps` from `--dvlt-steps` (`baselines.DVLT_STEPS`) [read] — `git diff main...dvlt-fewer-steps -- scripts/run_bench.py` in `/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-dvlt`.
- Our adapter builds `DVLT(**config.json)` with `decode_chunk_size=8, inference_steps=12`, calls `wrapper.model({"images": images})` directly, and recomputes poses with `rays_to_pose` under fp32 with uniform weights; native preprocessing is `dvlt.util.preprocess.preprocess_images` (longest side 504, centre crop to multiples of 14) [read] — `/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/scripts/baselines.py` L165-208. Measured inference: 3.9 GiB at 100 frames with chunk 8 (chunk 128 runs out of memory) [read] — `CLAUDE.md` (baselines notes).
- The attention uses `F.scaled_dot_product_attention` (flash, SDPA or FA3 backends), so global attention over S·P tokens is O(N) in memory [read] — `model_components/layers/attention.py` L21-78.
- Official per-GPU training load: ~20.7K patch tokens and up to 36 images per H100, with the encoder trained (LR 0.1×) and K up to 16 [read] — `dvlt-large.yaml`.

### Inferences
- [inferred, FLOPs] Analytic count at 24 views × 504² (1,301 tokens per view, d = 768). Encoder ≈ 218 MFLOP/token, ≈ 6.8 TFLOPs. One loop step ≈ 28 MFLOP/token in linear layers + 4 in frame attention + 96 in global attention (4·N·d with N = 31.2K) ≈ 128 MFLOP/token ≈ 4.0 TFLOPs. Decoders ≈ 0.6 TFLOPs (transformers) + ≈ 4.3 TFLOPs (conv depth head at full resolution). Total at K=16 ≈ 76 TFLOPs, which matches the paper's 75.9 and supports the K=16 reading in §3; K=12 ≈ 60, K=4 ≈ 28. Loop steps are about 85% of the FLOPs at K=16, and the global-attention share grows linearly with S. At 100 views × 977 tokens (504×378), one step is ≈ 32 TFLOPs against ≈ 20 TFLOPs for the encoder. Our measured ≈ 0.045 s/step is a median over mixed scene sizes, so it understates the per-step cost on 100-frame scenes.
- [inferred, memory] Fine-tuning the loop with the encoder frozen (run under `no_grad`) at S = 32, 504×378 (31.3K tokens):
  - state stored at each checkpointed call: 31.3K × 768 in fp32 ≈ 96 MB, so ≈ 1.5 GB for 16 calls and ≈ 0.4 GB for a 4-step student;
  - recompute of one `LoopedAABlock`: ≈ 2 GB (LN outputs, qkv, 4× MLP hidden, gate products, in bf16/fp32);
  - per-chunk decoder recompute: chunk 8 views with the full-resolution 64-channel conv stages, ≈ 3-4 GB;
  - weights 0.47 GB, plus Adam states for ≤ 30M trainable parameters ≈ 0.4 GB;
  - transient encoder forward ≈ 1-2 GB.

  Peak ≈ 8-12 GB. Without loop checkpointing, about 1.2 GB per step × K comes on top. A K=12/16 teacher pass under `no_grad` runs sequentially and peaks at about the inference figure (4.9 GiB at 24 views in the paper; 3.9 GiB at 100 frames measured).
- [inferred] Caching teacher targets offline is cheap if they are kept at patch resolution: final tokens, or rays at 36×27 per view. Full-resolution depth costs 504×378×4 B ≈ 0.76 MB per view. Caching DINOv2 tokens (32 views × 977 × 768 × 2 B ≈ 48 MB per sample) costs more storage than re-running the encoder, which takes about 0.4 s per scene.
- [inferred] Small-data pitfalls when fine-tuning: turn off `drop_path=0.1` (active in `model.train()`), keep the native preprocessing (resampling sensitivity at K<8), and keep a slice of K ∈ [8, 16] in the K sampler so that the K=12/16 behaviour is not overwritten.

### Gaps
- I did not run a fine-tuning step, so the memory estimate is unvalidated. A 5-minute profiling job on a 5090 would settle it.
- The `GenericDataset` fallback was not traced in detail. Whether any dataset other than ScanNet++ works without the private package is unverified.

## 5. Follow-up work citing DéjàView, and related looped / adaptive-depth work

### Takeaway
The main follow-up is **Solv3R** (UBC/Wayve/SFU/Toronto/Sony, arXiv 2611.02857, 2026-11-02). It fine-tunes DéjàView into a *stationary* solver: no step conditioning, with input injection, state normalisation, random-depth pre-calls without gradients, perturbation training, final-step supervision at a random call between 24 and 48, and distillation from DéjàView at 16 calls. This gives adaptive halting (stop when ∥∆z∥/∥z∥ < 0.02, 17.5 calls on average), warm starts and robustness to state perturbation, at the same 117M parameters and with ≈1 week on 8 H100s. Solv3R also shows that the released DéjàView breaks under perturbation, warm starts and mixed-progress states, which is a direct warning for "alternate loop and BA" ideas. Work on adaptive-depth LMs suggests that a short "finishing" phase of variable-depth training can re-tune a looped model's depth range, and that training-free extrapolation between early and late states can halve the number of loops.

### Cited Findings
- Solv3R initialises from DéjàView, uses AdamW at LR 2e-5 with EMA, 4-64 views, "16–40 solver calls without gradients, followed by 8 differentiable calls supervised at the final step", noise or view resets with probability 0.15, and λ_dist = 0.5 (distillation from DéjàView after 16 calls), trained for "approximately one week on 8×H100 GPUs" [read] — [Solv3R §3.4, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- Solv3R on the DéjàView 5-benchmark protocol (same 117M parameters): fixed 16 calls DTU 84.5/99.0, ETH3D 65.4/94.8, 7-Scenes 13.8/81.8, ScanNet++ 80.7/98.2, nuScenes 41.5/85.3 (AUC@3/@30). Adaptive (γ = 0.02, at most 64 calls): 85.5/99.1, 70.4/96.3, 14.2/81.9, 81.2/98.2, 41.5/85.3, at 91.3 vs 76.5 TFLOPs. DéjàView in the same table: 83.3/98.8, 66.0/95.4, 13.9/81.7, 79.5/98.0, 43.4/85.3 at 75.9 TFLOPs [read] — [Solv3R Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- Halting statistics: 144 of 148 windows meet the criterion within 64 calls, stopping after 13 to 32 calls (17.5 on average), "harder windows take more calls" [read] — [Solv3R §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- DéjàView's failure modes (from Solv3R): perturbing the state at call 8 and continuing for 8 calls leads to large rotation errors for DéjàView (Fig. 5 right shows values such as 6.3-15.6°, against 0.3-1.3° for Solv3R); warm-starting a sliding window "diverges"; with views at mixed refinement stages, DéjàView reaches pose AUC@3 58.7 (one clock) or 38.8 (per view) against 83.6 for Solv3R; reusing a single KV cache fails to relocalise (0% recall) [read] — [Solv3R Figs. 5-7, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857). Their explanation: "each state is tied to the iteration index at which it was computed" [read] — [Solv3R §3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- Solv3R ablations (each fine-tuned for 3k steps from DéjàView): DéjàView with their training but keeping the step conditioning gives AUC@5 73.5 at 16 calls, 19.3 at 128 and 0.0 at 1024. Step-conditioned gates "are sensitive to the planned refinement horizon P"; without input injection, accuracy at 1024 calls collapses to 0.0; without random-depth pre-calls, 3.4 at 128 [read] — [Solv3R Table 4, §5.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857). [inferred] 3k fine-tuning steps from DéjàView already change the dynamics measurably, which gives a useful scale reference for our budget.
- RAPTOR (Jacobs et al., ICLR 2026) showed that "trained Vision Transformers … layers can be faithfully approximated by a much smaller set of looped blocks, fit via post-hoc distillation against the original network". DéjàView adopts RAPTOR's gated block design. Starý et al. (2025) showed that DUSt3R's pointmaps refine progressively across decoder depth [read] — [DéjàView §2, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215).
- "Adaptive-Depth Is a Finishing Stage" (2610.14252, LMs): training at a fixed small depth for 67-80% of the run and then switching to uniform K ∈ {1..16} keeps NLL within 0.5-0.8% of full uniform-depth training. "After 16 uniform updates, the depth-averaged advantage of the K=16 update is no longer distinguishable from zero"; fixed-depth models "perform best near their respective training depths" [read] — [Piterbarg et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14252).
- LoopCD (Apple, 2610.02185, LMs): training-free contrastive extrapolation, final + ω(final − earlier), in logit or hidden space. At half the loop iterations it matches or exceeds full-depth accuracy, saving 22.5-48.2% of forward FLOPs [read] — [Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185).
- Other 2026 looped and adaptive-depth works found but only skimmed at abstract level: "Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part I: Topology, Input Injection, Recurrent-State Design" ([2610.23036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23036)); "Improving Test-Time Scaling with Adaptive Looped Transformers" ([2609.35748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748)); "One Block, Multiple Depths" (recurrent ViT, [2610.12448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448)); Looped DiT / LiFT for diffusion ([2609.40305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40305), [2610.05538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05538)). Adjacent 3D works citing the feed-forward line (not looped): CLoSeR ([2610.01927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01927)), PosePlaner, which denoises any feed-forward pose predictor ([2610.34845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845)), and OTT3R ([2609.36374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)) — [alphaXiv search results](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).

### Inferences
- [inferred] Solv3R occupies the "adaptive halting + continued refinement" niche for DéjàView, and its code is "will be made publicly available", i.e. not yet released. A SIGGRAPH contribution from us should therefore not be "make DéjàView adaptive". The open niche is cheap compute: few loop steps plus a classical or photometric TTO. Solv3R spends *more* calls than DéjàView (91.3 vs 75.9 TFLOPs) and has no TTO.
- [inferred] Solv3R's recipe (pre-calls without gradients + a few differentiable calls + final-step supervision + teacher distillation) is a memory-cheap template we can reuse for few-step training: only 8 differentiable calls in their case, and 2-6 in ours.
- [inferred] PosePlaner ("denoising any feed-forward pose predictor") may be a competing post-hoc refiner and is worth a look as a TTO-like baseline. I only saw the title and abstract.

### Gaps
- No other 2026 paper citing DéjàView was found that does step distillation or few-step DéjàView. The alphaXiv search returned only Solv3R as a direct follow-up, so citations from the last weeks may be missing.
- Solv3R's supplement (loss details, distillation loss form) was not read.

## 6. Hooks: properties that enable or block (a) step distillation, (b) adaptive halting, (c) loop–TTO coupling, (d) training early steps into the TTO basin

### Takeaway
(a) Few-step distillation is well supported by the code and needs little new structure: the step size dt is an explicit input to 0.32M parameters of gate MLPs, and K is a config knob. It needs no new architecture, only a teacher trajectory and a modest amount of unlabeled multi-view data. (b) Adaptive halting is structurally blocked in the released model (the schedule is fixed up front, there is no stationarity, and the model collapses beyond 16); Solv3R already solved it at large cost. (c) Feeding TTO results back into the loop is blocked without new training: there is no input channel, the poses come from a non-differentiable fit, and Solv3R shows that the released model does not recover from perturbed states. Coupling through the *differentiable decoders* is open, though. (d) Training the early steps for the TTO basin is feasible and is the most novel angle. Its key enablers are the differentiable ray, depth and camera readouts, `compute_world_rays` (which turns TTO-refined cameras into ray targets), and teacher-plus-TTO pseudo-labels that are stronger than the network teacher.

### Cited Findings
- The schedule is a constructor argument, the interval embedding takes any (t_now, t_next) ∈ [0, 1]², and gates are per-channel and zero-initialised — model.py L118-202, L464-550; `depth_scaling.py` [read]. Variable-K training on [8, 16] costs about 2-3% against fixed K at the same budget — [paper Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215) [read].
- A proper K-step schedule beats early-stopping (0.44 vs 0.31 at K=8) — [paper supp. E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215) [read]. There is no stationarity: the state norm grows, and the model diverges beyond K_max — [paper §3.4, supp. D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215) [read].
- `compute_world_rays(extrinsics_c2w, intrinsics, H, W)` builds the GT ray targets inside `train_step`, so any camera set (GT or TTO-refined) becomes a `RayLoss` target — model.py L927-937 [read]. The camera head is differentiable, but `rays_to_pose` is not — §1 [read].
- Released DéjàView fails under state perturbation, warm starts and mixed progress; fixing this took input injection, state normalisation and perturbation training, about one week on 8 H100s — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857) [read].
- Our in-house numbers (`local_measurements.md`, dev subset): TTO lifts K=12 56→71, K=8 44→67, K=6 28→57, K=4 8→31, K=3 2→19. AUC@30 stays at 72 at K=4 (full benchmark), so small-K outputs are coarse but not random [read, in-house].

### Inferences
**(a) Step distillation (few-step student from the K=12/16 teacher)**
- Enablers [inferred]:
  - The collapse at K<8 is out-of-distribution dt, so a student only has to learn what the gates should do at dt ∈ [0.2, 1]. That is the same structure as shortcut models or flow maps conditioned on (t, t+d): self-distil "one step of size 2d ≈ two teacher steps of size d" along the frozen teacher trajectory, matching the teacher's z at the matched t, or its decoded rays and depth.
  - Light-adapter ladder, cheapest first: (i) only the two `depth_scale` MLPs (0.32M); (ii) + LayerScale; (iii) LoRA on attn/MLP of the block; (iv) the whole block (14.5M). Encoder and heads stay frozen.
  - Shared weights: keep K ∈ [8, 16] in the training mix, or regularise the large-K outputs against the frozen copy, to preserve the teacher's behaviour. The "finishing stage" result suggests a short variable-K phase is enough to re-tune the depth range.
  - Data no longer has to be GT-labelled. The teacher can label any multi-view clip (ScanNet++ train split, DL3DV, CO3D, ARKitScenes …), which addresses the "10 scenes, data limited" failure of the October attempt. The earlier attempt trained the whole loop on 10 *benchmark* scenes, so that was a leakage risk too.
  - Memory per step is about 8-12 GB at 32 views (§4). With Solv3R-style no-grad pre-calls, only the student's 2-6 calls need gradients.
- Blockers / risks [inferred]:
  - Resampling sensitivity at K<8: train and test with the native `preprocess_images`.
  - The encoder is 74% of the parameters and costs ≈ 0.4 s, so even K=1 costs 0.42 s. A few-step student can at best roughly halve the forward time (0.96 s → ~0.55 s at K=4), and at K=16 the possible saving is larger.
  - Teacher-matching at patch level vs full resolution: matching tokens skips the 4.3-TFLOP conv head in the backward pass. Matching rays (already low-res and linear) is cheap.
- Cheapest first experiment (training-free) [inferred]: a LoopCD-style hidden-state extrapolation before decoding, z_K + ω(z_K − z_{K−1}), at K=4-6, plus non-uniform grids (the coordinator's probe, job 2832). Both are a forward-pass change only.

**(b) Adaptive halting per scene**
- Blockers [inferred]:
  - dt is fixed at the start, so halting can't change K mid-run without a schedule mismatch (Fig. 8 penalty).
  - The update norm decays because the schedule dictates it, not because the scene has converged.
  - Extrapolation beyond 16 collapses, so there is no "keep going on hard scenes".
- What remains possible [inferred]:
  - Choose K per scene *before* the loop, e.g. from a cheap proxy (number of views, the K=2 confidence map, disagreement between the camera head and the ray fit at a short probe).
  - Pick among {K=6, 8, 12, 16} schedules.
  - Use TTO's own convergence (photometric residual after T iterations) as the halting signal, a natural "BA decides" criterion.
  - Truly anytime refinement requires a Solv3R-style retraining, which is beyond our budget and already published.

**(c) Coupling the loop with our TTO**
- Blockers [inferred]:
  - There is no camera or depth input: z is tokens only, with no injection, so refined cameras can't be fed back without a new module. That module would be a Plücker/ray embedding added to the camera or patch tokens (as in pose-conditioned models), trained with the cameras of the K-step state perturbed.
  - Solv3R shows that the released model is not robust to edited states, so "loop k steps → BA → write back into z → continue" will likely break without perturbation training.
  - `rays_to_pose` sits under `no_grad`.
- Enablers [inferred]:
  - The decoders are differentiable and per-frame. A *latent-space TTO* is possible: optimise a small per-view offset of the camera token, or of z_K, through the ray decoder so that the decoded rays and poses minimise our photometric or feature-metric loss. That is a neural reparameterisation of BA with the network's prior, at the cost of a decoder pass per iteration.
  - Simpler and robust: keep TTO outside the loop (status quo). If anything is coupled, it is the *schedule* (K) and the *initialisation*, not the state.
  - A cheap test of whether alternation could work at all: decode at step k, run BA, compute rays from the BA cameras, re-encode them as rays-target and compare. Without an injection module, though, there is no write-back path.

**(d) Training early steps to land in the TTO basin**
- Enablers [inferred]:
  1. Teacher = DéjàView K=12/16 + photometric BA. That is a pseudo-label source stronger than the network alone (56→71 dev AUC@3), so a K=4-6 student is trained towards BA-refined poses (ray targets via `compute_world_rays`) and BA-corrected depth (network depth × refined log-depth grid). In the K=4-6 regime the student can in principle exceed the network teacher.
  2. Basin-shaped loss. Under our results, TTO fixes small pose and depth errors (K=8 → 67) but not gross ones (K=4 starts outside the basin except on DTU). So the student loss could be truncated or robust on pose error: penalise mainly errors above the basin radius (a few degrees), and leave fine accuracy to BA. This frees capacity for "be roughly right everywhere".
  3. Validate with the real criterion: AUC@3 *after* TTO at matched total time (forward + TTO), on held-out scenes, not network-only AUC.
  4. Cost: no differentiable BA in the training loop. Pseudo-labels are precomputed once per scene: ≈ 1 s forward + 0.3-1 s TTO on unlabeled scenes, so thousands of scenes take a few GPU-hours.
- Risks [inferred]:
  - BA pseudo-labels inherit TTO's failure modes (7-Scenes did not improve: 41.5→39.8 at K=12), so they need filtering, e.g. by photometric residual or teacher–TTO agreement.
  - The novelty claim has to be positioned against Solv3R (adaptive, more compute), OTT3R (feed-forward distillation, no TTO) and Test3R (test-time training).

### Gaps
- No paper or code evidence on how DéjàView behaves at K<8 after fine-tuning: no gates-only or LoRA few-step experiment exists in the literature I found. Feasibility of (a) at K=3-4 is therefore an open empirical question.
- Whether a latent-space TTO through the decoder converges faster than our explicit PBA is untested anywhere.
- The memory and FLOP numbers above are analytic estimates; a short profiling job would validate them.
