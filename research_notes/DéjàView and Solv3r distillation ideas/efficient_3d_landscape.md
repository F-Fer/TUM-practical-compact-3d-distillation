# Efficient / compact feed-forward multi-view 3D reconstruction (2025-2026) and small-scale distillation feasibility

Research notes compiled 2026-11-15 from arXiv (via alphaXiv full-text queries), dataset pages and the local DA3 benchmark config. "Abstract only" marks papers I saw only as titles/abstract snippets in search results, not read in full. Our own earlier results (knowledge base) are marked "(our KB)".

## 1. Distillation of 3D foundation models (OTT3R, Distill3R and others)

### Takeaway
Only one group (Leblanc & Poullis, Concordia) has published general-purpose single-workstation distillation of a multi-view 3D model: Distill3R (Fast3R to 72M, no pose head) and OTT3R (π³ to 102M, with SE(3) pose supervision). Both rely on an offline teacher cache. Both students fall far short of their teachers on out-of-distribution multi-view geometry. OTT3R shows that ~400 GPU-hours on 2×RTX 6000 Ada buys good in-distribution depth but poor OOD pose. Its main usable result is domain specialisation, which takes ~23 h on 2 GPUs. Nobody has distilled DA3, DéjàView or VGGT into a looped or fewer-step student, and nobody has distilled into a student that is meant to be refined by TTO.

### Cited Findings
- **OTT3R** (arXiv 2609.36374, ACCV 2026): teacher π³ (959M), student 102M (9.4× compression). Student = DUNE ViT-S encoder (21M), a 12-layer 512-d alternating-attention decoder, and three 4-layer heads (points, confidence, SE(3) camera). Up to 7× faster inference and 2-4× lower peak memory. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- OTT3R compute: 2× RTX 6000 Ada for ~8 days (192 h, i.e. ~384 GPU-h). The paper calls this "≈1.6% of VGGT's training compute" (64 A100 × 9 days), measured in TFLOPS·h. The 7-Scenes specialisation took 23 h (~0.2%). Pseudo-label cache: 667K images in 3.5 h on 2 GPUs (~23 img/s/GPU, π³ forward-bound), stored in 283 GB (geometry and confidence quantised, poses kept at full precision). — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- OTT3R data: 667K images, i.e. 33K 20-view samples, from CO3D, MegaDepth, ARKitScenes, ScanNet++, Habitat and BlendedMVS. 200 epochs, effective batch 16 (2/GPU × 4 accumulation), AdamW 1e-4, 518×224 resolution. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- OTT3R results: the general student zero-shot on 7-Scenes has Acc/Comp 10.16/34.84 cm, against 1.52/2.03 for the π³ teacher. The 7-Scenes-specialised student reaches 6.16/2.84. On DTU the zero-shot student gets 0.91/3.62 against the teacher's 0.11/0.13. Pose on CO3D is measured only as agreement with the teacher: RRA@30 96.1 (temporal pairs), 65.3 (strided pairs), 80.7 (all); median rotation 2.8° (temporal) vs 15.3° (strided). Authors: the student "does not replace the teacher on out-of-distribution multi-view geometry". — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- OTT3R ablations: removing camera (SE(3)) supervision degrades completion 17× (2.84 → 49.57 cm). Temporal-only sampling degrades it 9× (→ 25.22 cm). Scaling the encoder to DINOv2-B (280M) is *worse* at this budget (Acc 9.66 / Comp 20.97). Halving the student to 54M degrades slightly (6.62 / 3.19). "No choice we ablated closes the remaining gap to the teacher, which we attribute to the compute budget." No multi-seed runs. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- OTT3R latency at 518×224 on RTX 6000 Ada (N = 32/64/128 views): 0.24/0.68/2.20 s, 1.83/2.93/5.20 GB. The π³ teacher needs 1.49/4.30/12.82 s and 7.07/8.99/12.34 GB. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- OTT3R uses no test-time optimisation. It says inference-time accelerations (token merging, sparse attention, quantisation) can be stacked on the student. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- **Distill3R** (arXiv 2602.00865): teacher Fast3R (650M), student 72M (DUNE ViT-S + 6-layer/384-d fusion transformer + DPT heads), 5× faster. Trained 60 epochs in 2.875 days on 2× RTX 6000 Ada (~138 GPU-h). Cache: 450K images generated in 11.3 h on one GPU; images ~150 GB, and OTT3R puts the cache at ~600 GB. Same six training datasets as OTT3R; N = 20 contiguous frames. Student has no pose supervision and "fails to capture the required rotation to properly align views", so it is effectively monocular. 7-Scenes per-view Acc/Comp 8.76/8.88 vs teacher 2.45/2.97. — [Distill3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00865)
- Distill3R ablation: teacher-confidence weighting plus confidence distillation beats treating teacher outputs as plain pseudo-GT ("Labels-Only") on DTU (Acc 1.25 vs 1.37, Comp 1.54 vs 2.09). — [Distill3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00865)
- Other distillation work found (not general compact multi-view students): Foundry (2511.20721, distilling 3D foundation features for the edge; abstract only); "Geometric Foundation Model Distillation for Efficient Lunar 3D Reconstruction" (2607.01851, MASt3R; abstract only); SFE-VGGT (2609.36929, VGGT → event-camera monocular depth; abstract only); GRAFT (2610.02597, agglomerative distillation incl. MASt3R; abstract only); a 2024 Stanford DUSt3R KD report (2412.02039; abstract only). — [alphaXiv search results: Foundry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.20721), [Lunar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01851), [SFE-VGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36929), [GRAFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02597), [KD DUSt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02039)
- **Lite3R** (2605.11354) calls itself a teacher-student framework. In practice it is attention replacement (Sparse Linear Attention) plus FP8 QAT on a frozen backbone, with a small attention-distillation term (γ = 0.1). Trainable parameters: 3.1% of VGGT, 0.03% (0.11M) of DA3-Large. It is not a compact-student distillation. — [Lite3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11354)
- **Solv3R** (2611.02857) uses a distillation loss against DéjàView's 16-step predictions (λ_dist = 0.5, and the only loss on sparse/noisy datasets) while fine-tuning the shared block. It is self-distillation of a looped model, not compression. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- DéjàView cites RAPTOR (Jacobs et al. 2026): trained ViTs can be approximated by a much smaller set of looped blocks fitted by post-hoc distillation. DéjàView itself trains its looped block end-to-end with no teacher. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- Our KB: DA3/DéjàView students fine-tuned on 10 ScanNet++ scenes for ~600-1000 batch-1 steps were data/optimisation limited. Students trained on 8-16-view subsets improved at 32 frames but not on the 100-frame benchmark. DéjàView at K < 8 is so preprocessing-sensitive that cv2 resizing moved AUC@3 by ~10 points. — (our KB, `knowledge/ideas/distillation.md`)

### Inferences
- At our budget (~100-500 GPU-h), OTT3R is the realistic reference point. Distilling a new compact architecture from scratch at that budget gives a student that is much worse OOD than its teacher, and the DA3 benchmark (ETH3D, DTU, HiRoom) is largely OOD. Training a new student from scratch is therefore not a promising path for us. Starting from a pretrained compact model (DA3-SMALL/BASE, DéjàView 117M) and distilling *behaviour* (fewer loop steps, TTO-friendliness) avoids learning geometry from scratch.
- OTT3R's finding that pose supervision and wide-baseline sampling are "categorical" requirements implies that any student distillation must supervise cameras and sample wide baselines. This matches our KB finding that few-view training does not transfer to 100 views.
- Teacher caching is the standard trick: OTT3R stores ~0.42 MB/image. For DéjàView step distillation, caching the K=16 teacher's final tokens or decoded outputs, rather than re-running the teacher, would make training cheap.

### Gaps
- No public numbers on GPU-hours for the other distillation papers listed (abstract only).
- Whether OTT3R's student would close the gap after TTO was not tested by anyone.
- I found no paper distilling DA3 (any size) or DéjàView into a smaller or fewer-step model as of 2026-11-15.

## 2. Efficiency without (or with light) retraining: token merging, sparse attention, quantisation, pruning

### Takeaway
There is a crowded 2025-2026 literature, with >20 papers, nearly all targeting VGGT/π³ on *long* sequences (hundreds to thousands of frames). There they give 3-14× speedups. At ≤100 views (the DA3 benchmark) the training-free token methods give ≤~2× speedup or none, and they often cost pose accuracy. Layer pruning with closed-form linear recovery is the one training-free approach that helps at 30-100 frames (14-31% latency). It is also the one that leaves a compact-but-imperfect model which TTO could repair. Token, KV, quantisation and partitioning methods are not novel territory.

### Cited Findings
- **FastVGGT** (2509.02560, ICLR 2026): training-free token merging. On an L40S, VGGT at 100/200/500 frames takes 13.6/47.4/288.0 s, and FastVGGT 9.4/23.1/84.6 s. On 7-Scenes 100-frame prefixes: VGGT 8.12 s vs FastVGGT 8.15 s (no gain), with ATE 0.0293 → 0.0301. — [GoToHunt comparison tables](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892); [FastVGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02560)
- In the layer-pruning paper's 30-frame setting, FastVGGT cuts latency 14-19% but raises trajectory errors (VGGT APE 0.90 → 1.14 m, ARE 9.90 → 12.06°; π³ APE 0.36 → 0.51). — [Does the VGGT Family Need All Its Layers?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36842)
- **Block-sparse global attention / SparseVGGT** (2509.07120, CVPR 2026, RWTH): training-free. VGGT 500 frames 80.3 s (sparsity 50%) / 55.4 s (75%). At 100 frames 5.88 / 5.03 s vs 8.12 s, with ATE 0.0293 → 0.0329 / 0.0345. Sparse-π³ runs out of memory on 800-frame Bonn sequences in 48 GB. — [GoToHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892); [Block-sparse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07120)
- **LiteVGGT** (2512.04939, CVPR 2026): geometry-aware cached token merging. It needs retraining ("several days on a multi-GPU setup (8 high-performance GPUs)"). 500 frames 36.5 s, 100 frames 3.02 s (2.7×), with RPE-rot 0.318 → 0.365 at 100 frames. — [GoToHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892); [LiteVGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04939)
- **Co-Me** (CVPR 2026): confidence-guided merging with a learned controller, <1 h of training on one A100. Worse accuracy, e.g. 7-Scenes ATE 0.0870 vs 0.0698 at 500 frames. — [GoToHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892)
- **Speed3R** (2603.08055, CVPR Findings 2026): retrains π³ to be sparse (8 H20 GPUs per OTT3R). 500 frames 17.9 s vs π³ 106.5 s. — [GoToHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892); [OTT3R related work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- **Good Token Hunting / GoToHunt** (2605.23892, NeurIPS 2026; Toronto/Google/TUM): training-free hierarchical KV selection (diverse frames, then layer-adaptive intra-frame selection; first two global layers replaced by local attention). VGGT 500 frames 288 → 41.2 s (−85%) at equal or better accuracy. At 100 frames 8.12 → 6.96 s. Applied to DA3-Large at 500 frames: 96.5 → 29.2 s with ATE 0.0692 → 0.0677. Observation: VGGT/π³ are trained with ≤24 views, and accuracy is non-monotonic in the KV-frame budget (best at 40-60 frames). — [GoToHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892)
- **BlazeVGGT** (2610.29454): training-free frame-level KV sparsification chosen by camera-token DPP selection. 13.2× at 2,000 frames (1325 → 100 s, CD 0.418 → 0.328); 1.9× at 100 frames (8.1 → 4.2 s, CD 0.438 → 0.411) on ScanNet-50. It can be stacked on FastVGGT/LiteVGGT, but at short sequences "combining multiple aggressive acceleration strategies does not necessarily produce consistent improvements". — [BlazeVGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29454)
- **AVGGT** (CVPR 2026): replaces early global attention with frame attention and subsamples KV ("diagonal preservation and mean filling"). — [GoToHunt related work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892)
- **HTTM** (CVPR 2026, head-wise temporal token merging), **FlashVGGT** (CVPR 2026, compressed descriptor attention), **VGG-T³** (CVPR 2026, test-time training compresses KV; fast at 2,000 frames but CD 0.48 vs VGGT 0.42) — [BlazeVGGT refs and Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29454)
- **Quantisation:** QuantVGGT (Feng et al. 2025) does 4-bit PTQ. On 30-frame held-out sets it raised VGGT APE 0.90 → 1.12 and ARE 9.9 → 14.25. Its public implementation has no INT4 deployment. Also QVGGT (2605.31124), tail-aware PTQ (2602.01741), "Rotation Is Token-Blind" PTQ criterion (2611.07955) (abstract only). — [Layer pruning paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36842); [Distill3R related work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00865); [QVGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31124); [Rotation Is Token-Blind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07955)
- **Lite3R** (2605.11354): SLA plus FP8-aware QAT (1 epoch; a 20-epoch schedule drifted). On an A100, VGGT 483 → 274 ms (1.76×) and 5.7 → 2.5 GB; DA3-Large 187 → 95 ms (1.97×) and 2.7 → 1.4 GB. Pose degrades: DTU64 rotation error VGGT 0.38 → 0.70°, DA3-L 0.93 → 1.58°. — [Lite3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11354)
- **Layer pruning** (2609.36842, "Does the VGGT Family Need All Its Layers?", UQ/TU Delft): 3,018 interval deletions on VGGT, π³ and VGGT-Ω. Removable layers cluster in a broad *early* region and a narrow late region. Degradation is approximately additive (search goes from O(L⁴) to O(L²)), and CKA is a cheap proxy. Closed-form, token-type-aware linear recovery is fitted on 100 calibration scenes in seconds; calibration takes <1 h per backbone with CKA, ~6-17 h with measured degradation, on an RTX 6000 Ada. Result at 30 frames: π³ 36 → 20 layers (−44.5% aggregator params, −30.9% latency, −18.5% memory) with APE 0.36 → 0.37. VGGT-Ω 48 → 30 layers. VGGT tolerates only 10/48 removed (APE 0.90 → 1.12). It generalises to 100 and 200 frames (π³ at 100 frames: 4.70 → 2.99 s). — [Layer pruning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36842)
- Other 2026 efficiency papers (abstract only): Spark3R (asymmetric token reduction), PrePARE (pre-AA token pruning), TurboVGGT (adaptive alternating attention), RegimeVGGT (layer-wise redundancy removal), ReSS and VASC (sparse attention), Zebra3D (AMD, layerwise sparse hybrid), VGGT-Prime (compute-adaptive mixture-of-heads), GPA-VGGT (geometry-preserving acceleration), LoG-VGGT, Diversity-aware View Partitioning, Prune-and-Merge. — [Spark3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06270), [PrePARE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08371), [TurboVGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14315), [RegimeVGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18439), [ReSS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35593), [VASC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01013), [Zebra3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12727), [VGGT-Prime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23733), [GPA-VGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37000), [LoG-VGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21437), [View Partitioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01885)
- Memory-only engineering: VGGT-X drops unused intermediate layers (only layers 4/11/17/23 feed the heads), uses bf16 and chunked frame ops to scale VGGT to 1,000+ images "without compromising performance". The same idea as our `da3_lowmem`. — [VGGT-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25191)

### Inferences
- For our regime (24-100 views, DA3-SMALL..GIANT, DéjàView), token/KV methods buy little: FastVGGT gives ~0% at 100 frames and GoToHunt ~14%. Proposing "another token-reduction method" would be neither novel nor useful. Such methods could be an orthogonal add-on but not a contribution.
- Layer pruning plus linear recovery is cheap and directly produces "compact models of chosen size from a big one". Pairing it with our 0.2-0.3 s TTO is a natural, untested combination: TTO can probably correct exactly the small pose degradations pruning causes.
- The early-redundancy finding (VGGT family) is interesting next to DéjàView's recurrence: early global layers do little cross-view work. This is consistent with GoToHunt's observation that early-layer attention is near-uniform.

### Gaps
- No paper reports these methods on DA3-SMALL/BASE or on DéjàView (only DA3-Large in GoToHunt and Lite3R).
- Speedups at 24 views (DéjàView's reference setting) are not reported anywhere I found.

## 3. Looped / recurrent / weight-shared and solver-in-the-loop 3D models

### Takeaway
DéjàView (May 2026) is the only looped multi-view reconstruction model trained from scratch. Solv3R (Nov 2026) fine-tunes it into a stationary solver with adaptive stopping. Other "solver-in-the-loop" work is either two-view (BA-T), feed-forward 3DGS (GradSplat, DeltaSplat) or classical BA around feed-forward priors (BLASt3R). Looped-transformer theory and training-free looping are active in LLM/ViT land but not yet applied to 3D reconstruction models, apart from DéjàView and Solv3R.

### Cited Findings
- **DéjàView** (2605.30215, NeurIPS 2026, NVIDIA): DINOv2 ViT-B encoder plus one shared alternating frame/global block applied K times, conditioned on the time interval (t_k, t_k+1) through zero-init MLP gates (s_attn, s_mlp, s_out). Two 2-block 384-d decoders (ray, depth) and a camera MLP head; 117M params. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- DéjàView training: 128 H100s, V ∈ [2, 18] views at 504 px, ~2.5M tokens/step (up to 4,608 images), 200K iterations plus 40K depth-head finetune, 29 public datasets with √N temperature sampling. K ~ Beta(2,1) on [8, 16] during training; K_inf = 16 in the main tables. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- DéjàView step count (Table 5, 100K-iteration ablation models): the variable-K model scores AUC@3 29.6 at K_inf = 12 and 33.3 at K_inf = 16 (fixed-K = 12: 30.4; fixed-K = 16: 33.8). The recurrence "does not extrapolate beyond its step range": AUC@3 peaks near K_max then degrades. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). The user's note "default 12, collapses below K=6" comes from our own measurements/config, not from the paper. The paper's main tables use 16 (possible discrepancy with the released config).
- DéjàView analysis: the state norm grows, but cos(z_k, z_16) → 1 and the relative update norm decays ~5× ("directional refinement"). Global attention concentrates on corresponding patches over iterations ("emergent iterative correspondence search"). Weight sharing beats a decoupled 16-block variant (AUC@3 30.2 vs 23.0). — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- DéjàView efficiency (24 views, A100): 75.9 TFLOPs, 4.9 GiB, average IR 80.3, AUC@30 91.8. Comparisons: VGGT 190 TFLOPs / 14.7 GiB; π³ 153.8 / 6.6; DA3-L 71.4 / 7.3; DA3-G 178.7 / 13.0; VGGT-Ω-1B 99.8 / 7.9. MASt3R-SfM FLOPs (1150) "cover only the pair-network forward passes, excluding the iterative global alignment". — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- **Solv3R** (2611.02857, UBC/Wayve/Sony): replaces DéjàView's step-conditioned gates with constant gates, injects z_0 at every call, and RMS-normalises the state. This gives a *stationary* operator that can continue from any state, add views, and reuse one KV cache. Trained to be stable (P1), corrective after noise/reset (P2) and warm-startable (P3). It fine-tunes only the block, gates and projection (encoder and heads frozen). Training: ~1 week on 8×H100, 4-64 views, 16-40 no-grad calls plus 8 differentiable calls, perturbation probability 0.15. Ablations use 3k steps. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- Solv3R results: at 16 calls it matches DéjàView. Adaptive stopping (relative state update < 0.02, max 64 calls) adds only 3-4 calls on average and improves most metrics (e.g. ETH3D AUC@3 66.0 → 70.4). The released DéjàView collapses when iterated past its range: AUC@5 76.5 at 16 calls, 0.0 at 128 and 1024. Solv3R gets 79.1 and 78.8. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- **BA-T** (2606.03287, TUM/ETH): an iterative transformer for *two-view* bundle adjustment (abstract only). — [BA-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287)
- **GradSplat** (2610.38951): puts gradients of the per-scene objective inside the forward pass of a feed-forward 3DGS predictor (abstract only). **DeltaSplat** (2610.09853): iterative Gaussian refinement for pose-free FF-3DGS (abstract only). **iLRM** (cited by DéjàView): treats unshared layers as optimisation steps for FF-3DGS. — [GradSplat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38951), [DeltaSplat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09853), [DéjàView related work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- **PosePlaner** (2610.34845): denoises any feed-forward pose predictor from pairwise planar geometry (abstract only). — [PosePlaner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845)
- Generic looped-transformer work (abstract only): *Training-Free Looped Transformers* (2605.23872; loops a mid-stack block of a frozen LLM checkpoint at inference); *One Block, Multiple Depths* (2610.12448; a recurrent ViT block matches full-depth encoders without feature distillation); *Recursive CLIP* (2610.34563; distils CLIP into looped blocks); *Adaptive Depth in Looped Transformers* (2607.20519; learned halting gates); *UpLoop* (2610.25004; upcycling LLMs into loops); *Rotate the Loop* (2610.14030). — [Training-Free Looped](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23872), [One Block](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448), [Recursive CLIP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34563), [Adaptive Depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20519), [UpLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25004), [Rotate the Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14030)

### Inferences
- "Looping makes K a compute knob" (DéjàView), "stationary looped solver with adaptive halting" (Solv3R) and "RAPTOR-style post-hoc looping of a ViT" are all taken.
- Open: (a) fewer-step DéjàView via step/trajectory distillation (DéjàView's variable-K only covers [8, 16], and below 8 quality drops sharply); (b) halting or step-allocation driven by an *external* geometric signal (photometric or reprojection residual of our TTO) rather than Solv3R's latent update norm; (c) training-free or cheaply calibrated looping of DA3's cross-view layers.
- Solv3R's recipe shows that fine-tuning only the shared block from DéjàView, with frozen encoder and heads, is stable and changes behaviour within ~3k steps (its ablations). That is a useful template for a step-distillation project on our 5090s.

### Gaps
- Solv3R's 3k-step ablation compute in GPU-hours is not stated (only "approximately one week on 8×H100" for the full run).
- I did not find a paper that combines a looped 3D model with classical TTO/BA.

## 4. Streaming / test-time-training models (compute vs quality)

### Takeaway
Streaming models (CUT3R, TTT3R, Spann3R, Point3R, StreamVGGT, LingBot-Map) trade accuracy for constant per-frame cost. They are a different operating point (per-frame ms, long video) from our offline ≤100-view setting. Solv3R shows that a looped offline solver can match or beat them per frame.

### Cited Findings
- Solv3R streaming table (per-frame time / peak memory, with ATE on Sintel / TUM-dyn / ScanNet): CUT3R 52 ms / 6.1 GB (ATE .204/.170/.843); TTT3R 52 ms / 6.1 GB (.200/.112/.416); Spann3R 78 ms / 2.6 GB; Point3R 192 ms / 3.5 GB; LingBot-Map 119 ms / 14.3 GB (.149/.032/.089); VGGT-Long 584 ms / 19.9 GB; Solv3R 95 ms / 5.0 GB (.157/.015/.077). With 16-call pipelining, Solv3R processes 16× more frames per second than DéjàView at the same latency. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- TTT3R (2509.26645, ICLR 2026) recasts CUT3R's state update as test-time training to extend length generalisation (abstract only). — [TTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26645)
- VGG-T³ compresses VGGT's KV through test-time training. Fast at 2,000 frames but less accurate than VGGT (ScanNet-50 CD 0.48 vs 0.42). — [BlazeVGGT Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29454)
- Other related work (abstract only): CLoSeR (2610.01927, long-context streaming with loop closing), Mamba3R (2611.04221, fixed-capacity state memory), Scal3R and tttLRM (test-time training for large-scale reconstruction). — [CLoSeR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01927), [Mamba3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04221), [GoToHunt refs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892)

### Inferences
- These models are not direct competitors on the DA3 benchmark (offline, ≤100 views). They matter mainly as evidence that "state + iterative update" designs are mainstream, and that streaming is already claimed by Solv3R.

### Gaps
- No streaming model reports results on the DA3 benchmark protocol that I found.

## 5. Feed-forward prediction + optimisation at matched compute; which initialisation errors optimisation can correct

### Takeaway
Many hybrid systems (VGGT+BA, VGGT-X, BLASt3R, MASt3R-SfM, Glob3R, VGGT-SLAM, ...) report that BA/global alignment on top of feed-forward predictions improves pose. They report cost as wall-clock minutes (often 10-100× the forward pass) or as FLOPs that exclude the optimisation. None I found sweeps model size × optimisation iterations at a matched total budget, or asks whether a *smaller* model plus fast TTO matches a *larger* model. The only "which errors can optimisation fix" evidence is anecdotal: VGGT-X finds that large pose deviations are not corrected, and SelfEvo and Solv3R report that a decent initialisation is needed.

### Cited Findings
- **VGGT-X** (2509.25191): VGGT forward (memory-efficient) 1.29 min on MipNeRF360 scenes. Adaptive epipolar global alignment (XFeat matches, 300 iterations) takes 1.78 min total and lifts AUC@30 0.951 → 0.988. Full VGGSfM-style BA takes 157 min and reaches 0.994 (40 GB A100). After joint pose and 3DGS optimisation, "most residuals cluster near zero... the model struggles to sufficiently correct poses with large deviations". VGGT's own tracking head as the correspondence source was much worse than XFeat (AUC@30 0.920 vs 0.988). — [VGGT-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25191)
- **BLASt3R** (2609.05210, NAVER): a regularised BA over adjustable depth maps (a monocular network predicts m corrective depth channels; BA optimises α, β per view) plus a fast multi-view matcher, on a GPU LM solver. ETH3D-MVS mAA@30: BLASt3R 98.5 (98.8 with COLMAP BA), VGGT 75.7, VGGT-BA 88.4, MASt3R-SfM 81.5. Runtime on Tanks&Temples at 25/50/100/200 frames: VGGT-BA 1.5/3.5/12/75 min (no fine tracking) or 6/9/42.5/140 min (with fine tracking), BLASt3R 0.5/0.75/1.2/2.1 min. It plots an accuracy-vs-time trade-off on ETH3D but does not vary the network size. — [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210)
- The VGGT paper reports that BA on IMC raises AUC@3 from 39.2 to 66.4 at about +1.6 s (0.2 → 1.8 s). This number comes from the project CLAUDE.md summary of the paper and was not re-verified this session. — [VGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11651)
- **DéjàView Table 3** is the closest "compute-matched" comparison: TFLOPs and peak memory at 24 views against IR/AUC@30. It covers feed-forward only, and MASt3R-SfM's FLOPs exclude its optimisation. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- **Solv3R** frames extra solver calls as test-time compute (adaptive stopping, +3-4 calls average). It does not compare against classical TTO. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- **OTT3R** reports training compute in TFLOPS·h relative to VGGT. Inference is reported as latency and memory at N = 32/64/128. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- **Free Geometry** (2604.14048): label-free test-time LoRA adaptation of DA3-GIANT and VGGT, ~2 min per *dataset* on one RTX Pro 6000. Average gains on DA3-benchmark datasets (ETH3D, ScanNet++, 7-Scenes, HiRoom): +3.73% pose and +2.88% point maps (VGGT, 32 views). DA3-G gains shrink with more views (AUC@3 at 100 views: ETH3D 0.485 → 0.522, ScanNet++ 0.842 → 0.843, 7-Scenes 0.308 → 0.298). Hyperparameters are tuned per dataset. — [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048)
- Other hybrids (abstract only): Glob3R (2607.09225, global SfM with 3D foundation models), GRF-Recon (2609.20012, global ray-field optimisation), VGGT-SLAM 2.0, F² SLAM, VGGT-GS SLAM, "Does VGGT Know What It Does Not Know?" (2610.35806, pose uncertainty), GeoCond (2609.18465, reliability adapter for low overlap/parallax failures). — [Glob3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09225), [GRF-Recon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20012), [VGGT uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35806), [GeoCond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18465)
- Our KB: on dev, photometric TTO at T = 25 costs 0.2-0.3 s and lifts AUC@3 SMALL 16.2 → 43.8, BASE 27.1 → 59.2, LARGE-1.1 48.6 → 71.0, GIANT-1.1 67.8 → 74.9. At matched time, a model + TTO beats the next larger model alone. — (our KB, CLAUDE.md "TTO status")

### Inferences
- The matched-compute, model-size × TTO-iteration frontier on a standard benchmark appears unpublished, and our KB already has it. Hybrids report time, but always for one network.
- Reporting suggestion consistent with prior work: give TFLOPs (DéjàView style) *and* wall-clock on named hardware including the TTO (BLASt3R/VGGT-X style). Many papers omit the optimisation's cost from FLOPs, which a matched-compute paper can explicitly fix.
- "Which errors can TTO correct" is under-studied: VGGT-X's residual histogram is the only explicit analysis found. A systematic basin-of-convergence analysis (initial rotation/translation/depth-scale error vs post-TTO error, per model size) would be new.

### Gaps
- I did not re-read the VGGT paper or MASt3R-SfM/Light3R-SfM this session for their exact timing tables.
- No paper found that quantifies a "convergence basin" of photometric or feature-metric BA when initialised from feed-forward predictions of different quality.

There is plenty of non-overlapping data. ## 7. Fine-tuning budgets reported for VGGT / π³ / DA3 / DéjàView

### Takeaway
Light-touch adaptation is cheap: minutes (test-time LoRA), hours (linear recovery, a few-k-step block fine-tune), or about a GPU-day per few GPUs (encoder LoRA). Full student training or retraining with token merging needs multiple GPU-weeks. Our 100-500 GPU-h matches OTT3R/Distill3R/Fin3R-scale projects, or ~1/3 of Solv3R's full run.

### Cited Findings
- **Pre-training scale, for reference:** VGGT 64 A100 × 9 days; DéjàView 128 H100s × 200K+40K iterations; DA3 128 H100s × 200k steps; MapAnything 64 H200 × 10 days (420K steps); VGGT-Ω 128 H100s. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374); [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215); [DA3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647); [MapAnything](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13414)
- **Solv3R** (fine-tune DéjàView's shared block + gates + projection; encoder and heads frozen): ~1 week on 8×H100 (~1,300 H100-h). Stage 1 ~70k iterations at batch 8 windows; stage 2 ~20k iterations at batch 32 windows; 4-64 views. Ablations at 3k steps. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- **OTT3R:** ~384 GPU-h (2×RTX 6000 Ada × 192 h) for a 102M student from scratch, 20-view samples. Domain specialisation 23 h on the same pair. — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- **Distill3R:** ~138 GPU-h (2× RTX 6000 Ada × 2.875 days), 20-view samples. — [Distill3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00865)
- **Fin3R** (NeurIPS 2025): encoder-only LoRA (rank 8, with weight re-normalisation) on VGGT/DUSt3R/MASt3R/CUT3R, decoder frozen. 4× L20 for one day (~96 GPU-h). Per epoch: 20K SA-1B images (MoGe pseudo-labels) + 1K Hypersim + 1K TartanAir; 10 epochs; 2-8 views; effective batch 64. Fine-tuning the decoder with monocular data hurt multi-view matching. VGGT ScanNet1500 AUC@5 28.4 → 35.2; DTU Acc 1.187 → 0.948. — [Fin3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22429)
- **SelfEvo:** VGGT/π³, camera decoder frozen, everything else trained. 20 epochs × 50 steps = 1,000 optimisation steps, EMA teacher (decay 0.995 VGGT, 0.99 π³). GPU type and hours not found. — [SelfEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08532)
- **Free Geometry:** LoRA rank 32 on DA3-GIANT/VGGT plus a trainable camera token. 5 epochs, batch 4, 5-20 samples per scene, ~2 min per dataset on one RTX Pro 6000. — [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048)
- **Layer pruning + linear recovery:** 100 calibration scenes of 30 frames, closed-form ridge fit in seconds. CKA selection <1 h per backbone on an RTX 6000 Ada. — [Layer pruning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36842)
- **Lite3R:** 1 epoch of FP8-aware QAT on a single A100; only SLA projection layers trained. Dataset size and hours not stated in what I read. — [Lite3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11354)
- **Co-Me:** <1 h on one A100. **LiteVGGT / Speed3R:** "several days on a multi-GPU setup (8 high-performance GPUs)". **Reliev3R:** 64 NPUs (per OTT3R). — [GoToHunt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23892); [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374)
- **Our KB:** DA3 fine-tuning needs small learning rates (5e-6 backbone, 2e-5 heads at batch 1); ~1000 batch-1 steps on 10 scenes was insufficient. — (our KB)

### Inferences
- With 4× RTX 5090 at 6 h per job, ~100-500 GPU-h is enough for: (a) a Solv3R-ablation-scale (≥3k steps) fine-tune of DéjàView's shared block for a new objective (e.g. step distillation) with cached teacher targets; (b) Fin3R-scale LoRA on DA3-SMALL/BASE; (c) many training-free pruning/looping calibrations. It is not enough for a from-scratch student that beats the teacher OOD (OTT3R's lesson).
- Training at many views (≥32-64, as Solv3R does with 4-64) matters for transfer to 100-view evaluation. Our KB and OTT3R both point the same way. Memory at 64 views for a 117M looped model with 8 differentiable calls should fit 32 GB with checkpointing (inference: 4.9 GiB at 24 views); this is an estimate, not measured.

### Gaps
- No GPU-hour figures for SelfEvo, PURE (2610.14698), 3R-Adapter (2611.11964) or Test3R; not retrieved.
- RTX 5090 vs RTX 6000 Ada / H100 throughput equivalence for these workloads not measured. OTT3R cites 182 bf16 TFLOPS for the RTX 6000 Ada; I did not verify the 5090 figure.

## 8. Novelty gaps reachable with ~100-500 GPU-h (as of 2026-11-15)

### Takeaway
The open space is at the *interface* between compact/looped feed-forward models and classical TTO. That covers: distilling for few loop steps, training to be "TTO-correctable", allocating budget between loop steps and TTO iterations, and using TTO as a teacher. Pure efficiency (tokens, quantisation, layer pruning) and pure looping (DéjàView, Solv3R) are covered.

### Cited Findings (closest prior work per gap)
- Few-step looped models: DéjàView variable-K covers only K ∈ [8, 16] and degrades outside it [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). Solv3R distils toward DéjàView-16 for stability, not for fewer steps [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857). RAPTOR fits looped blocks to ViTs post hoc (cited in [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)).
- External-signal halting: Solv3R halts on latent update norm [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857); VGGT-Prime is compute-adaptive mixture-of-heads (abstract only) [VGGT-Prime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23733); adaptive-depth looped transformers are LLM work [Adaptive Depth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20519).
- Matched-compute FF+TTO: DéjàView Table 3 (FF only), BLASt3R time-accuracy plot (one network), VGGT-X timings — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215), [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210), [VGGT-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25191).
- TTO as teacher: SelfEvo (context-asymmetry self-distillation, no optimisation) [SelfEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08532); Free Geometry (feature-level TTA) [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048); DA3 (teacher depth aligned to sparse GT) [DA3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647); OTT3R (network teacher cache) [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374); PURE (unlabelled post-training; abstract only) [PURE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14698).
- Pruning + TTO: layer pruning with linear recovery [Layer pruning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36842); Lite3R [Lite3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11354).
- Training-free looping: Training-Free Looped Transformers (LLMs) [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23872); One Block Multiple Depths (ViT) [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448).
- Optimisation-aware feed-forward: GradSplat (gradients inside the forward pass, FF-3DGS) [GradSplat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38951); BA-T (two-view iterative BA transformer) [BA-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287); BLASt3R adjustable depth basis [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210).

### Inferences (the gaps)
1. **Few-step DéjàView via trajectory/step distillation, evaluated with TTO.** Distil the K=16 trajectory or endpoint into K = 2-6 (fine-tune the shared block and gates only, encoder and heads frozen, teacher outputs cached). Then measure whether K=4 + photometric TTO matches K=16 at lower total time. Not published. The closest work (DéjàView, Solv3R, RAPTOR) does not target K < 8 or pair with TTO. Budget: Solv3R-ablation scale (~3k+ steps), tens to low hundreds of GPU-h. Risk: our KB shows DéjàView at K < 8 is very preprocessing-sensitive.
2. **"TTO-friendly" distillation / training for correctability.** Fine-tune a compact model (DA3-SMALL/BASE or few-step DéjàView) with a loss on the *post-TTO* result. Options: unroll a few differentiable Gauss-Newton/LM steps of the photometric BA, or penalise only error components TTO cannot remove (e.g. large rotations and scale-inconsistent depth, which VGGT-X found hard to correct), so the student spends capacity where TTO is weak. Closest: GradSplat (FF-3DGS), BA-T (two-view), BLASt3R (adjustable depth basis, but supervised). Not found for multi-view pose/depth models.
3. **Budget allocation between learned iterations and classical iterations, with a geometric halting signal.** For a fixed time budget, choose K loop steps vs T TTO iterations per scene, using the photometric/reprojection residual (or TTO's convergence) as the stop rule instead of Solv3R's latent norm. Closest: Solv3R adaptive stopping, VGGT-Prime. Cheap if run on the released DéjàView/Solv3R checkpoints (inference only) plus our TTO.
4. **A systematic matched-compute frontier and convergence-basin analysis on the DA3 benchmark.** Model size (DA3 S/B/L/G, DéjàView K, pruned π³/DA3) × TTO iterations, with total FLOPs and wall-clock including TTO. Plus "which initialisation errors TTO corrects": initial vs post-TTO error per error type, per model. Closest: DéjàView Table 3, BLASt3R Fig. 1, VGGT-X residual histogram. We already have most of the data. Mostly analysis compute.
5. **Optimisation-as-teacher self-distillation on unlabelled video.** Run DA3/DéjàView + our photometric BA on unlabelled scenes (ScanNet++ train minus benchmark IDs, DL3DV, ARKitScenes), cache the refined poses and depth, and fine-tune the compact model on them (OTT3R-style cache, SelfEvo-style recipe with the camera head trainable because BA fixes the pose targets). Goal: a model that needs fewer TTO iterations, or reaches post-TTO quality feed-forward. Closest: SelfEvo, Free Geometry, DA3 teacher-alignment, OTT3R. None uses an optimiser's output as the teacher for a multi-view model, as far as found. Budget: Fin3R/OTT3R scale (~100-400 GPU-h).
6. **Layer-pruned or training-free-looped DA3 plus TTO.** Prune early cross-view layers of DA3-BASE/LARGE or π³ with linear recovery (100 calibration scenes, <1 h), or loop a mid-stack DA3 block training-free to create a compute knob. Then show TTO recovers the lost accuracy, giving a continuous model-size axis for the matched-compute curve. Closest: the layer-pruning paper (no TTO, no DA3), Training-Free Looped Transformers (LLMs only), Lite3R. Very cheap.
7. **Correspondence/feature-metric TTO that reuses the looped model's own attention.** DéjàView's global attention converges onto correspondences over iterations ("emergent iterative correspondence search"), and VGGT-X found VGGT's tracking head too unreliable for alignment. Testing whether DéjàView's late-iteration attention or features give TTO-grade correspondences (replacing RoMa/XFeat at no extra network cost) appears unpublished. Closest: DéjàView App. C, RoMa-Ω (2609.09507, what feed-forward 3D models know about matching; abstract only) [RoMa-Ω](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09507), VGGT-X. Inference-only.

### Gaps
- Novelty claims rest on alphaXiv/arXiv searches up to 2026-11-15. Papers in submission (e.g. CVPR 2027) may appear any week, so re-check gaps 1, 2 and 5 before committing. Solv3R's follow-ups and NVIDIA's own DéjàView follow-ups are the main threat to gap 1.
- I did not check whether PURE (2610.14698) or 3R-Adapter (2611.11964) use optimisation outputs as pseudo-labels; both were seen only as titles. They are the closest unread candidates for gap 5.
