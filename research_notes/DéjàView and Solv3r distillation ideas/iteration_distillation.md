# Reducing or adapting the number of loop steps in weight-tied networks (step distillation, adaptive computation, fixed-point acceleration), mapped to DéjàView + TTO

Scope note: these notes combine a literature survey (alphaXiv full-text reads and web search, 2026-11-15) with facts read directly from the DéjàView code and checkpoint config in this repository and from our own measurements (`knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md`). Local sources are linked by path. Classical papers that were **not** re-read in this session are marked "(not re-read)"; for these only the core mechanism is stated, without numbers.

## 1. Diffusion / flow step distillation: what transfers to a deterministic weight-tied refinement loop, and what does conditioning on step size / remaining steps buy?

### Takeaway
DéjàView's loop is already parameterised like an interval-conditioned flow map: every application of the shared block is conditioned on the pair (t_k, t_{k+1}). Shortcut, flow-map and progressive self-distillation therefore carry over almost literally, and the tiny time-gate MLP (about 0.32M parameters) is a natural adapter. The released checkpoint was trained only with K in [8, 16], so our collapse at K ≤ 6 is very likely in large part an out-of-distribution step-size problem. That is exactly the failure that shortcut-style training fixes in other domains. Distribution-matching and adversarial distillation target multimodal outputs, which a near-deterministic regressor doesn't have, so they are a low priority.

### Cited Findings

#### DéjàView internals that decide the mapping
- During training, K is drawn per batch from Beta(2,1), scaled and rounded into [K_min, K_max] = [8, 16], so E[K] ≈ 13.3. The block is applied along a uniform partition of [0, 1], and each step is conditioned on its interval (t_k, t_{k+1}). The paper runs K_inf = 16 and reports "degradation observed when K_inf is pushed substantially outside" the trained range. — [DéjàView, arXiv 2605.30215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- The released checkpoint config has `num_steps 16`, `min_steps 8`, `k_sampler_beta_a 2`, `k_sampler_beta_b 1`, `k_sampling "linspace"`, `time_conditioning "interval"`, `recurrence_mode "gated"`, but **`inference_steps 12`**. The paper's experiments use 16, while our benchmark ran the config default of 12. — [checkpoint config.json](/rhome/pr26rb00/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--nvidia--dvlt/snapshots/20f919875a00ee3ab2e840f2f31580f4652b7c5e/config.json)
- In the code, the inference grid is `ts = torch.linspace(0, 1, K)`, and step i is conditioned on (ts[i], ts[i+1]). The last step uses `t_next = 1.0` with t_now = 1, a "terminal-step sentinel (delta_t = 0)". So K = 8 means steps of 1/7, K = 16 steps of 1/15, K = 6 steps of 1/5, K = 4 steps of 1/3 and K = 3 steps of 1/2. — [third_party/dvlt/src/dvlt/model/dvlt/model.py](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/third_party/dvlt/src/dvlt/model/dvlt/model.py) (`_solve_inference_linspace_k`, `_solve_train_linspace_k`)
- Gating: z' = z_k + s_attn ⊙ LS(Attn(LN(z_k))), z'' = z' + s_mlp ⊙ LS(MLP(LN(z'))), z_{k+1} = s_out ⊙ z''. The scales come from s = 1 + MLP(γ(t_k, t_{k+1})), where the MLP is zero-initialised and γ concatenates sinusoidal embeddings. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215). In code the MLP is Linear(128→64), SiLU, Linear(64→3·768), about 158k parameters, with one module in each of the two sub-blocks (frame attention and global attention), so about 0.32M parameters in total (my count). — [depth_scaling.py](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/third_party/dvlt/src/dvlt/model/dvlt/depth_scaling.py), [blocks.py](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/third_party/dvlt/src/dvlt/model/dvlt/blocks.py)
- On the choice of conditioning, the authors write: "Conditioning on continuous time, rather than on a discrete step index … decouples the block from any specific value of K". Only the final state is supervised, which "spar[es] (K−1) decoder forward and backward passes". — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- The recurrence is *not* a fixed point. ‖z_k‖ grows monotonically, cos(z_k, z_16) rises toward 1, and the relative update ‖Δz‖/‖z‖ decays from about 0.5 to about 0.1 ("directional refinement"). Global attention concentrates on corresponding patches over the iterations ("emergent iterative correspondence search"). — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- Step-count ablation (100K-iteration models, mean of 5 benchmarks): fixed K = 12 gives AUC@3 30.4, fixed K = 16 gives 33.8, and the variable-K model gives 29.6 at K_inf = 12 and 33.3 at K_inf = 16. — [DéjàView Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- Our sweep (DA3 benchmark, mean AUC@3 of 5 datasets) gives K = 1: 0.0, K = 2: 0.2, K = 3: 2.1, K = 4: 9.0, K = 6: 31.1, K = 8: 47.6, K = 12: 58.7. Median forward times are 0.42 / 0.52 / 0.56 / 0.66 / 0.76 / 0.96 s for K = 1 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 12 on tum2 (RTX 5090). — [knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md)
- Our photometric TTO (0.2–0.3 s) lifts dev-subset AUC@3 from 28 to 57 at K = 6 and from 56 to 71 at K = 12, but fails at K ≤ 4. — project brief (measurements by the team)

#### The step-distillation family
- Progressive distillation trains the student so that one of its steps reproduces two teacher sampler steps, and repeats this halving. — [Salimans & Ho, arXiv 2202.00512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00512) (not re-read)
- Consistency models learn f(x_t, t) that maps every point of a PF-ODE trajectory to its origin, either by distillation from a teacher ODE solver (CD) or by training without a teacher (CT). — [Song et al., arXiv 2303.01469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01469). Follow-ups: improved CT, [arXiv 2310.14189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14189); ECT, [arXiv 2406.14548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14548); continuous-time sCM, [arXiv 2410.11081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11081); Consistency Trajectory Models (arbitrary t→s jumps), [arXiv 2310.02279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02279) (all not re-read)
- Shortcut models condition the network on the step size d as well as on t, and use the self-consistency target "one 2d-step = two d-steps". A single network trained in a single phase serves any budget. — [Frans et al., arXiv 2410.12557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12557) (not re-read; its use in looped LMs is confirmed by LoopFormer, below)
- Flow maps Φ_{t→r}: flow map matching, [arXiv 2406.07507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07507); learning flow maps by self-distillation, [arXiv 2505.18825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18825); Align Your Flow, [arXiv 2506.14603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14603); MeanFlow learns the average velocity over (r, t) through the MeanFlow identity and gives 1-NFE generation trained from scratch, [arXiv 2505.13447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13447) (not re-read)
- "Depth as time" classifies one-step models into three groups: boundary-anchored (t→0, consistency), interval-conditioned (t→r, MeanFlow) and step-conditioned (t→t−d, shortcut). It points to compositional consistency, Φ_{t→r} = Φ_{s→r} ∘ Φ_{t→s}, as the structure such models learn. It compresses a MeanFlow SiT-L/2 (459M) into **one weight-shared block conditioned on an interval (τ_i, τ_{i+1})** (27.6M, 16.6× fewer parameters), with **data-free** training from the teacher's hidden states h_1…h_L. Training over coarse intervals cuts block applications: half as many gives FID 5.25 and a third as many gives 6.11, against 4.01 for the teacher (after post-training). "Simply running the native-depth student with fewer applications degrades FID", and removing the interval conditioning "more than doubles FID-50k" on CIFAR-10. — [Asiimwe et al., arXiv 2610.03626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03626)
- Rectified flow / reflow straightens trajectories by retraining on (noise, sample) pairs that the teacher generates. — [arXiv 2209.03003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.03003); InstaFlow, [arXiv 2309.06380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06380). Direct one-step distillation of DDIM endpoints: [Luhman & Luhman, arXiv 2101.02388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02388) (not re-read)
- Distribution-matching distillation: DMD, [arXiv 2311.18828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18828); DMD2, [arXiv 2405.14867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14867). Adversarial: ADD, [arXiv 2311.17042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17042); LADD, [arXiv 2403.12015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12015). Guided distillation, which folds CFG in before progressive distillation: [arXiv 2210.03142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03142) (all not re-read)
- A deterministic geometry example: single-step Marigold depth works once a DDIM scheduler issue is fixed, and end-to-end fine-tuning of the single-step model with a task loss beats the multi-step pipeline. — [Garcia et al., arXiv 2409.11355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11355) (not re-read)

#### These ideas already transferred to looped transformers (language)
- **LoopFormer** (ICLR 2026) conditions each loop on (t, Δt) through zero-initialised AdaLN gates. Its loss is L_L + λ1·L_S + λ2·L_cons, where L_S is the task loss of a sampled shortcut trajectory and L_cons = ‖stopgrad(h^(L)) − h^(S)‖² aligns short and full trajectories (λ1 = λ2 = 0.1). The extra trajectory costs about 1.5× training FLOPs. At a 12× budget (M = 4 loops of 3⊗8) it reaches Pile PPL 11.12, against 11.66–12.18 for early-exit baselines. At the most aggressive 6× budget, however, a naive early-exit loop (12.61) beats it (14.30). At fixed M = 4, the schedule choice spreads perplexity by about 1.4, and the best schedules use "larger steps early and finer steps late". — [Jeddi et al., arXiv 2602.11451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11451)
- **Rectified Looped Transformer** uses remaining-time feedback, x_{k+1} = (1−η_k)·x_k + η_k·h_{k+1} with η_k = 1/(K−k), which adds no parameters. With K_train = 8, inference at K_test = 2 gives Pile PPL 13.85 against 21.60 for direct feedback, and K_test = 32 gives 17.72 against 57.72. — [Li et al., arXiv 2611.16265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16265)
- Solv3R already distils DéjàView's 16-step predictions (λ_dist = 0.5) into a new operator. — [Kwak et al., arXiv 2611.02857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)

#### What step conditioning buys, and what it costs
- Gains in the DéjàView block ablation (mean AUC@3): shared block 30.2, + time-conditioned residual gates 31.5, + state gate 33.3. — [DéjàView Table 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- Huginn found that a step-conditioned core block "interacts badly with path independence, leading to models that cannot extrapolate". — [Geiping et al., arXiv 2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)
- Solv3R makes a related point. Because DéjàView's update depends on τ_k, "one application of F_θ thus cannot jointly update views that have received different numbers of iterations". DéjàView "loses reconstruction accuracy when iterated beyond its trained range" and diverges when warm-started. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- In ELR, conditioning on loop index and budget leaves one-loop loss unchanged, lowers loss at every additional loop, and can be grafted onto a trained checkpoint with zero-initialised heads. — [Ladhak et al., arXiv 2610.12582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12582)

### Inferences
- In flow-map terms, DéjàView is an interval-conditioned flow map over latent tokens whose only "teacher trajectories" were uniform grids of 8–16 steps (Δ ∈ [1/15, 1/7]). Below K = 8 the gate MLP is queried at Δ ∈ {1/5, 1/3, 1/2}, which it has never seen. Two causes overlap there: unseen step sizes and fewer block applications. Grid manipulations at inference can separate them at zero training cost (Section 5, combination 1).
- The cleanest transfer is shortcut / progressive self-distillation with the model's own K = 12/16 trajectory as the teacher. The target of a student step (t, t+2Δ) is two teacher steps (t, t+Δ), (t+Δ, t+2Δ) in latent space. Alternatively, use LoopFormer-style endpoint consistency, z_{K_s} ≈ stopgrad(z_16), or decoded-output distillation as in Solv3R. The teacher is the model itself, so **no GT is needed**: any unlabeled multi-view collection works. That removes the data limit that sank the earlier 10-scene fine-tuning ([knowledge/ideas/distillation.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/distillation.md)).
- "Depth as time" is the closest existence proof that matching hidden states, data-free, can train one interval-conditioned block that runs fewer, coarser applications with moderate loss. It also shows that naively running fewer applications without training on coarse intervals degrades quality, which is what our K-sweep shows.
- Distribution matching and adversarial losses address multimodality and blur in generation. Given its images, DéjàView's target is essentially unique, so DMD reduces to regression; an adversarial depth loss is optional polish at best. Reflow corresponds to endpoint distillation on (z_0, z_16) pairs. Garcia et al. suggest adding the true task loss (GT where available) on top of teacher matching.
- The payoff is bounded by wall-clock time. From our forward times, one loop step costs about (0.96 − 0.42)/11 ≈ 0.05 s at benchmark scale, and the fixed cost (DINOv2 encoder + decoders) is about 0.37 s. Going from K = 12 to K = 4 saves at most about 0.4 s, and K = 6 to K = 4 only about 0.1 s. Since K = 6 + TTO already matches K = 12 raw (57 vs 56 dev AUC@3), the stronger lever may be **raising the quality at K = 6–8 toward K = 12–16**, then spending the saved time on TTO, rather than pushing below K = 4.
- Our K = 12 baseline may not be DéjàView's best operating point. The paper uses K_inf = 16, and its Table 5 gains 3.7 AUC@3 from 12 to 16. Our sweep stops at 12, so K = 16 should be run before any distillation claim.

### Gaps
- I found no published work that applies shortcut, consistency or flow-map step distillation to a looped 3D reconstruction or pose model (alphaXiv search, 2026). The nearest are Solv3R's endpoint distillation of DéjàView and "Depth as time" for images.
- It is unknown whether K ≤ 4 is fundamentally limited. If the loop performs a sequential correspondence search, there may be a minimum number of applications (compare the "hardness" lower end of the extrapolation window in Section 2). DéjàView reports no fixed-K training below 12.
- I didn't re-check the exact numbers of the classical diffusion-distillation papers; only their mechanisms are used here.

## 2. Looped / recurrent-depth transformers: training with random loop counts, extrapolation, halting

### Takeaway
Training with random loop counts is the standard recipe (Huginn, DéjàView, ELR, LoopFormer). Recent evidence (October 2026) says depth elasticity can be added in a **short finishing stage** on an already trained model, at about 16–45% extra compute or less. Extrapolating beyond the trained maximum is unreliable unless the operator is stationary and path-independent. Halting either uses zero-shot convergence tests (KL between successive steps, relative state-update norm), which presuppose an operator that is stationary or nearly so, or learned halting (ACT, PonderNet, MoR). Because DéjàView's time grid depends on K, it cannot exit mid-trajectory: the budget must be chosen up front, or checked with a draft-and-verify scheme.

### Cited Findings
- Universal Transformers pair weight sharing across depth with per-position Adaptive Computation Time. — [arXiv 1807.03819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819); ACT, [arXiv 1603.08983](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983); PonderNet's probabilistic halting with a geometric prior, [arXiv 2107.05407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05407); CALM's confidence-based early exit, [arXiv 2207.07061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07061); Mixture-of-Recursions' per-token recursion depth through a router, [arXiv 2507.10524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10524); Relaxed Recursive Transformers, which convert a pretrained model into a recursive one with layer-wise LoRA, [arXiv 2410.20672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20672) (all not re-read; LoopFormer reports that "naively transplanting these techniques into looped architectures is fragile" and that early-exit loops stagnate, [arXiv 2602.11451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11451))
- Huginn (3.5B) samples the recurrence count per step from a log-normal Poisson distribution (mean about 33, heavy tail) and backpropagates only through the last 8 iterations (truncated BPTT). A random initial state s_0 plus input injection at every step gives path independence. Its zero-shot adaptive exit stops when the KL divergence between successive steps falls below 5·10⁻⁴; the mean steps to exit vary by MMLU category from 12.7 to 16.2. Warm-starting from the previous token's final state cuts 1–2 steps, and the model can self-speculate by drafting with fewer iterations. — [Geiping et al., arXiv 2502.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171)
- Deep-thinking recurrent nets: training with random unrolling, input injection ("recall") and a progressive loss lets them extrapolate to more iterations without "overthinking". — [Schwarzschild et al., arXiv 2106.04537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04537); [Bansal et al., arXiv 2202.05826](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05826) (not re-read; cited by DéjàView and Huginn)
- Learning to Extrapolate in Depth (theory on graph connectivity): a block trained at depth K has a *finite* window of useful test depths. "Too few test-time loops provide insufficient propagation, while too many eventually create false connections", and the lower end is set by the instance's hardness. — [Yang et al., arXiv 2610.14456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14456)
- Adaptive-Depth Is a Finishing Stage: training at a fixed depth for the first 67–80% of the run, then uniformly over K = 1…16, saves 45% of modeled compute at 200M parameters with mean NLL within 0.5%, and 31–37% at 1B. "After 16 uniform updates, the depth-averaged advantage of the K=16 update is no longer distinguishable from zero". — [Piterbarg et al., arXiv 2610.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14252)
- ELR (NVIDIA): mixing depths only in the final 25% of pretraining costs about 1.16× the baseline's compute, against 1.62× when mixing throughout (4B). Loop counts must be mixed *within* one optimizer update (one-loop rank override) to keep the one-loop endpoint; synchronized one-K-per-step sampling falls short of the baseline. "A model trained at one fixed loop count reaches the lowest loss at that count and degrades at every other k", so elastic models trade some per-depth optimum for flexibility. — [Ladhak et al., arXiv 2610.12582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12582)
- DéjàView itself: "the trained recurrence does not extrapolate beyond its step range: a few channels diverge once K_inf exceeds K_max". Looped-transformer stabilisation (Yang et al. 2024) "plateau[s] past the trained budget … at significant additional training cost". Variable-K training matches but doesn't exceed fixed-K training. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- Solv3R stops adaptively when the relative state update r_k = ‖z_{k+1} − z_k‖/‖z_k‖ falls below γ = 0.02. On 148 windows, 144 stopped within 64 calls, after 13–32 calls (17.5 on average), with "harder windows tak[ing] more calls". Adaptive stopping improves nearly all metrics over its fixed 16 calls "with only 3–4 additional solver calls on average". — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- In our K sweep, the AUC@3 ratio of K = 6 to K = 12 differs strongly by dataset: 7-Scenes 21.7/29.7 = 0.73, HiRoom 44.1/76.6 = 0.58, ETH3D 19.6/35.9 = 0.55, DTU 40.0/79.5 = 0.50, ScanNet++ 30.4/72.0 = 0.42 (ratios are my arithmetic). — [knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md)
- DéjàView builds on RAPTOR (Jacobs et al., 2026), which fits the L layers of a trained ViT with "a much smaller set of looped blocks … via post-hoc distillation against the original network". Starý et al. (2025) show that DUSt3R's decoder refines pointmaps progressively across depth. — cited in [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)

### Inferences
- For DéjàView, the training-time knob is K_min. A short finishing fine-tune that samples K from about {2…16} instead of [8, 16] fits the Piterbarg/ELR evidence that elasticity is cheap to add late. On one GPU, gradient accumulation over micro-batches with different K reproduces ELR's "within-step mixing" at no extra cost.
- Huginn and Solv3R show the tension: step or budget conditioning (DéjàView, LoopFormer, ELR) helps **fewer** steps and budget control, while stationarity (Huginn, Solv3R) helps **more** steps, extrapolation, warm starts and convergence-based halting. Our goal is fewer steps, so DéjàView's interval conditioning is an asset to keep, not a flaw to remove.
- DéjàView's options for halting:
  - choose K per scene before the loop with a cheap predictor (number of views, overlap or encoder statistics);
  - draft and verify: run K = 4, and if it disagrees with a second cheap estimate (e.g. pose consistency, or the photometric cost after a few TTO iterations), re-run at K = 8–12, in the spirit of CALM and Huginn's self-speculation;
  - after shortcut training, use a non-uniform "anytime" schedule: coarse early steps, then decide whether to fill the remaining interval with fine steps (LoopFormer finds coarse-early / fine-late best).

  Per-token halting (ACT/MoR) doesn't fit: all tokens share t, and global attention couples them.
- The large spread of the K = 6/K = 12 ratio across datasets (0.42–0.73) and the "hardness" theory both suggest that an oracle per-scene K would save real compute. This can be measured from the existing per-scene outputs without any training.

### Gaps
- No 3D-specific halting literature turned up beyond Solv3R's relative-update rule and DEQ-Stereo's residual-norm rule.
- No paper tests how far *below* K_min a looped model can go after a short finishing stage; the LM papers vary K from 1 upward within their training range.

## 3. Deep equilibrium models: can a looped model be turned into a DEQ cheaply, and does that reduce iterations?

### Takeaway
DEQs mainly save training memory. Iterations are saved only through fixed-point acceleration (Broyden or Anderson; learned solvers give about 2×), Jacobian regularisation, or warm starts. DéjàView is not a fixed-point iteration (its norm grows and its update is step-conditioned), so DEQ acceleration doesn't apply to it directly. Solv3R (November 2026) already turned DéjàView into a stationary solver, but it took about a week on 8×H100 and uses *more* calls (17.5 on average) than DéjàView's 12. The DEQ route therefore buys anytime refinement, adaptive stopping and warm starts, not fewer steps. The cheap opportunity is inference-only acceleration or warm-starting on Solv3R, once its weights are public.

### Cited Findings
- DEQs find the fixed point with a root solver (Broyden) and backpropagate through the equilibrium analytically with implicit differentiation, at constant memory. — [Bai et al., arXiv 1909.01377](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377) (summary via [search result](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8358-deep-equilibrium-models))
- Neural Deep Equilibrium Solvers use a learned network that generalises a learnable form of Anderson acceleration, trained unsupervised. They add about 0.9–1.1% to the DEQ's training time, use 1–3% of its size and give about a 2× speedup. — [Bai, Koltun & Kolter, ICLR 2022](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/5919)
- Jacobian-free backprop, [arXiv 2103.12803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12803); phantom gradients, [arXiv 2111.05177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05177); Jacobian regularisation for stability and fewer solver steps, [arXiv 2106.14342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14342) (all not re-read)
- DEQ-Flow recasts the RAFT update operator as a DEQ and reuses "both the predicted flow and hidden state from the previous frame to accelerate convergence". — [DEQ-Flow, arXiv 2204.08442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08442), as described in [DEQ-Stereo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39701)
- DEQ-Stereo (2026) uses Broyden with Armijo line search and stops adaptively (‖g‖ < 30 at inference, or 16 iterations), plus an auxiliary loss on one random intermediate state. A **naive DEQ plug-in of a ConvGRU updater degraded accuracy and increased runtime** because the update converged unstably; a simplified gated unit (ConvSGU) fixed this. On TartanAir its full model reaches EPE 0.39 in 0.125 s, against 0.40 in 0.253 s for the iterative baseline. Warm-starting from the previous frame's hidden state cuts runtime by 34%, while reusing the explicit prediction (DEQ-Flow style) can hurt (Table 8). Training: 8× TITAN RTX, 200K steps. — [Han et al., arXiv 2610.39701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39701)
- DéjàView: "Unlike deep equilibrium networks, our recurrence does not converge to a fixed point in feature space". — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- **Solv3R** = DéjàView turned into a DEQ-like stationary solver. Its changes:
  - **Architecture.** Step-conditioned gates are replaced by learned constant gates, initialised as the average of DéjàView's 16 gate values. z_0 is re-injected through a zero-initialised linear map W at every call, and the state is renormalised to a fixed RMS (ρ ≈ 0.85).
  - **Training.** 16–40 calls without gradient, then 8 differentiable calls supervised at the last one. With probability 0.15 the state is first perturbed by Gaussian noise (σ ∈ [0, 0.5]·RMS) or partial view resets. A distillation loss on DéjàView's 16-step outputs is added (λ = 0.5).
  - **Cost.** The encoder and heads are frozen; only the block, gates and W are trained, at lr 2e-5 for the block and 2e-4 for the gates. Training runs about 70k + 20k iterations, "approximately one week on 8×H100".
  - **Results.** Fixed 16 calls roughly match DéjàView. Adaptive stopping averages 17.5 calls and gains a little. Warm starts reach accurate cameras "in fewer solver calls than restarting from scratch", whereas DéjàView diverges under warm starts.

  — [Kwak et al., arXiv 2611.02857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- For a time-independent, nonexpansive network, RLT's remaining-time schedule is a Krasnosel'skii–Mann iteration with fixed-point residual ≲ dist(x_0, Fix)/√(log K). "Fixed-Point Reasoners" (Movahedi et al. 2026, arXiv 2606.18206) use damped feedback to promote convergence. — [RLT, arXiv 2611.16265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16265)

### Inferences
- A cheap DEQ conversion of DéjàView is conceivable: the Solv3R recipe at a smaller scale, i.e. constant gates + z_0 injection + RMS renorm, with only gates, W and a LoRA trained on teacher pseudo-labels with truncated BPTT. It doesn't serve our objective, though. Solv3R needs more calls than DéjàView, and DEQ-Stereo shows that naive conversion of a non-contractive updater hurts.
- Warm starts are the DEQ property that is genuinely relevant to TTO. A stationary operator could restart from a state that encodes an *improved* estimate (TTO-refined poses, or a cheap DA3-SMALL prediction). That needs a geometry → state injection path, which neither DéjàView nor Solv3R has.
- If Solv3R's weights become public, Anderson or Broyden acceleration of its calls is a zero-training experiment. The DEQ literature suggests about 2× fewer calls, i.e. roughly 8–10 instead of 17.5, but this is an extrapolation, not a measurement.

### Gaps
- Solv3R's code and models are announced ("will be made publicly available") but I didn't verify that they are released.
- No measurements of Anderson or Broyden acceleration on looped multi-view transformers were found.

## 4. Iterative refinement in vision (RAFT, pose refinement, learned optimisers, DROID-SLAM): how were iterations reduced, and who alternates learned and classical steps?

### Takeaway
In flow, stereo and SLAM, iteration counts came down mainly through better initialisation (SEA-RAFT regresses an initial flow directly), warm starts (DEQ-Flow, DEQ-Stereo), adaptive termination (DEQ) and more informative residuals inside the loop (BA-T). Explicit "iteration distillation" of RAFT-type models is essentially absent from the literature. Alternating learned updates with classical optimisation is established: DROID-SLAM's update operator plus dense BA, BA-Net/PixLoc's learned-feature LM, and diffusion-aided BA. It has not been done inside a looped multi-view transformer. BA-T and PosePlaner (2026) are the nearest 3D works.

### Cited Findings
- RAFT refines a flow field with a weight-tied recurrent unit, so the number of iterations can be changed at inference. A follow-up ECCV 2022 supplement chose 12 rather than 32 refinement steps. — [RAFT, arXiv 2003.12039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12039); [web search summary](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136950298-supp.pdf)
- A targeted web search for distilling RAFT iterations into fewer steps "didn't turn up a paper that directly distills RAFT's iterations into fewer refinement steps". — [search](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2003.12039)
- SEA-RAFT "directly regresses an initial flow" (fewer iterations needed) and trains with a mixture-of-Laplace loss. — [arXiv 2405.14793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14793)
- BA-T (TUM, NeurIPS 2026) uses one shared BA-inspired layer that computes a "latent residual" between camera-transformed geometry tokens and refines camera and geometry tokens. Training: K = 5 with per-iteration supervision weighted α^{K−k}, α = 0.8; 2 days on 8×H100. Results:
  - Most of the gain arrives in 3–4 iterations.
  - The latent residual is "the dominant factor": the crudest residual alone recovers 76% of the rotation gain over a conventional iteratively trained decoder.
  - Starting from the same initialisation, BA-T beats DROID-SLAM's classical BA: rotation AUC@5 0.68 vs 0.53, translation AUC@0.05 m 0.42 vs 0.08, in 31 ms vs 161 ms.

  — [Zhang et al., arXiv 2606.03287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287)
- PosePlaner refines any feed-forward pose with a single Euler step of a flow-matching adapter, conditioned on plane-warping residuals. On ScanNet1500, from LoFTR pre-BA poses, rotation AUC@5 goes 11.5 → 13.4 with BA → 29.5 with PosePlaner. From an accurate MASt3R initialisation it is at parity or slightly worse ("not guaranteed to improve an already-near-optimal estimate"). — [Chen et al., arXiv 2610.34845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845)
- Diffusion pose models: PoseDiffusion, "Solving pose estimation via diffusion-aided bundle adjustment" (ICCV 2023), [arXiv 2306.15667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15667); RayDiffusion, [arXiv 2402.14817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14817); DiffusionSfM, [arXiv 2505.05473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05473). These are titled and characterised in PosePlaner's related work as "visual-prior-driven, with no explicit per-correspondence geometric channel". As I recall it (not re-read), PoseDiffusion's sampler also takes guidance steps on an epipolar (Sampson) error.
- Learned-update + classical-solver hybrids (not re-read): DROID-SLAM alternates a ConvGRU update operator with a differentiable dense-BA layer (Gauss–Newton on poses and inverse depth) in every iteration, [arXiv 2108.10869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869); BA-T calls such systems ones that "integrate learned priors with differentiable bundle adjustment" ([arXiv 2606.03287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287)). DPVO, [arXiv 2208.04726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04726); BA-Net (learned features + differentiable LM), [arXiv 1806.04807](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.04807); PixLoc, [arXiv 2103.09213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09213); DeepIM (iterative render-and-compare pose refinement), [arXiv 1804.00175](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.00175); LS-Net (learned least-squares solver), [arXiv 1809.02966](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02966); Theseus (differentiable nonlinear least squares), [arXiv 2207.09442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09442); Deep Declarative Networks, [arXiv 1909.04866](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04866); Diffusion Posterior Sampling (measurement-gradient guidance between learned denoising steps), [arXiv 2209.14687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687)
- Solv3R can pipeline views through the window (a new view enters and the oldest leaves after each call) and reuses one KV cache for fixed map views, which gives "16× more frames per second" than re-solving DéjàView per frame at equal latency. — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857)
- DéjàView contrasts itself with RAFT-style task-space refinement, which decodes and supervises after every step, while DéjàView refines an internal state and supervises only the end. It also cites iLRM (Kang et al. 2026), which treats successive unshared layers as optimisation steps for feed-forward 3DGS. — [DéjàView](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30215)
- Test-time training for 3D: Test3R tunes DUSt3R prompts with a cross-pair consistency loss, [arXiv 2506.13750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13750) (repo CLAUDE.md); TTT3R is cited in PosePlaner as a streaming 3D test-time-training model ([arXiv 2610.34845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845)).

### Inferences
- Three ways to couple DéjàView loop steps with our photometric TTO:
  - (a) **Sequential** (today's setup): the loop only needs to land inside the TTO basin.
  - (b) **Latent geometry guidance at inference** (DPS / PoseDiffusion style): between the last loop steps, set z_k ← z_k − η·∇_z L_photo(Dec(z_k)), with gradients through the small decoder (2 blocks at 384 dims plus heads) and our photometric/NCC loss. This needs no training.
  - (c) **Learned injection** (DROID-SLAM / BA-T style): decode, run a few PBA iterations, and feed the correction (Δpose, Δdepth, or the photometric residual itself, BA-T's "latent residual") back into the camera tokens through a zero-initialised adapter, training the adapter and gates with the block frozen.
- BA-T's ablation (explicit residuals matter more than iteration itself) is evidence that putting a *measured* geometric residual into the loop could let fewer loop steps do the same job, because the loop no longer has to compute the correspondence evidence implicitly.
- PosePlaner's regression at accurate initialisations mirrors our TTO behaviour: refinement helps most when the start is mediocre but inside the basin. Coupling and halting policies should therefore be conditioned on the initial quality.

### Gaps
- No RAFT/stereo iteration-distillation paper was found (search above). That is either a real gap or a sign that better initialisation made it unnecessary.
- I found no work that interleaves classical BA or photometric steps *inside* a looped multi-view transformer, or that applies DPS-style guidance to a deterministic looped regressor.

## 5. Per-technique cost and feasibility, prior use in 3D, and the 3–5 most promising combinations for DéjàView + TTO

### Takeaway
Under our budget (one RTX 5090, 6 h jobs, a few nodes, little GT), the techniques that fit are (i) gate-only interval/shortcut self-distillation trained on the model's own pseudo-labels, (ii) inference-time manipulations (time grids, latent guidance, per-scene K selection) and (iii) analyses that reuse existing runs. Solv3R-style DEQ conversion (about 1 week on 8×H100) and from-scratch looped training (128 H100s for DéjàView) are out of reach. Already done in 3D: the K knob with variable-K training (DéjàView), a stationary solver with adaptive stop and warm starts (Solv3R), an iterative latent BA layer (BA-T), DEQ stereo/flow, and one-step flow-matching pose refiners (PosePlaner). Not found: few-step distillation of a looped 3D model, TTO-aware ("basin") distillation, geometry-guided looping, or joint allocation of compute between loop steps and TTO steps.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
Below are five candidate combinations, ranked by novelty × feasibility. Each has a cheapest first experiment.

**1. "ShortLoop": interval/shortcut self-distillation of DéjàView's time gates, extending K_min from 8 down to 3–4.**
- *Mechanism:* keep DéjàView's (t_k, t_{k+1}) conditioning and train only the two IntervalDepthScaling MLPs (~0.32M params; optionally plus a LoRA on the shared block). The losses are:
  - endpoint consistency: student z_{K_s} for K_s ∈ {2…8} ≈ stopgrad(teacher z_16 or z_12), or decoded rays/depth/cameras as in Solv3R;
  - shortcut self-consistency: one step over (t, t+2Δ) ≈ two teacher steps.

  Sample K_s per micro-batch and accumulate across several K (ELR's within-step mixing). The teacher is the frozen model itself, so unlabeled multi-view data works, from datasets *outside* the DA3 benchmark scenes.
- *Cheapest first experiment (zero training, minutes on the dev subset):*
  - (i) run K = 16, the paper's default;
  - (ii) truncated in-distribution grids: run the K = 8 (Δ = 1/7) grid but stop after m = 3/4/6 steps, then apply the (1, 1) sentinel step, and compare with the uniform K = m grid;
  - (iii) non-uniform schedules that use in-range Δ early and one large final jump.

  If (ii) beats the uniform small-K grid, out-of-distribution conditioning explains part of the collapse, and gate-only training is the right adapter. *Next step:* gate-only training on one 5090 for under 6 h. My estimate is a few thousand steps at 16–32 views, with the teacher's z_16 cached offline (≈2 MB per view in fp16; my estimate). Evaluate AUC@3 at K = 3/4/6/8 with and without TTO.
- *Novelty:* shortcut consistency for looped models exists only in LMs (LoopFormer, trained from scratch). I found no looped-3D step distillation. *Risk:* if the loop's correspondence search really needs sequential applications, K ≤ 4 may stay poor (theory in [2610.14456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14456)). Because the fixed cost is ~0.37 s, the realistic win is K = 6 reaching K = 12 quality.

**2. Basin-aware (TTO-aware) few-step distillation.**
- *Mechanism:* the student doesn't need to match the teacher. It only needs to land inside the photometric BA's basin of convergence, so the loss puts weight where TTO fails:
  - a truncated or robust pose loss that is zero below a basin radius (e.g. a few degrees) and steep above it; or
  - differentiate through a few unrolled Gauss–Newton/LM steps of our PBA (Theseus/DDN style), so that the loss is measured *after* TTO.
- *Cheapest first experiment (no training):* using the TTO-on-DéjàView runs at K = 3–12 (already submitted per [dejaview-steps.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md)), plot per-scene initial rotation/translation error against TTO success. This gives the basin radius and shows which error modes (gross flips, scale, focal) put K ≤ 4 outside it. That calibrates the truncation threshold for the loss of combination 1.
- *Novelty:* I found no distillation objective in 3D or elsewhere that targets the downstream optimiser's basin rather than the teacher output. It also fits the matched-compute question directly.

**3. Geometry-guided looping (DPS-style latent guidance with the TTO objective).**
- *Mechanism:* at the last one or two loop steps, take a gradient step on the latent state against our photometric/NCC loss evaluated on the decoded geometry (z_k ← z_k − η∇_z L(Dec(z_k))), then continue looping. This interleaves "learned step" and "optimiser step" without any training, analogous to measurement guidance in diffusion samplers.
- *Cheapest first experiment:* dev subset, K = 6 and K = 12, guidance at the step before last, on a coarse pyramid level to keep the decoder backward pass cheap. Compare against loop-then-TTO at matched wall-clock.
- *Novelty:* guidance exists for diffusion pose models (PoseDiffusion lineage) but not for a deterministic looped multi-view transformer. *Risk:* the decoders are pre-norm, and the state norm grows, so step sizes in latent space may need normalising.

**4. Per-scene K with TTO-aware halting (draft and verify).**
- *Mechanism:* run K = 4–6 first. If a cheap check fails, re-run at K = 8–12. Candidate checks: the photometric cost after a few TTO iterations, disagreement between decoded poses at two coarse grids, or Solv3R-style relative update norm. Alternatively, after combination 1, use a coarse-early / fine-late schedule that can be extended.
- *Cheapest first experiment (no training):* from the existing per-scene K-sweep and TTO metrics, compute the oracle per-scene K* (smallest K whose K + TTO reaches K = 12 + TTO) and the compute it saves. Then test which observable predicts K*. The dataset-level ratios (7-Scenes 0.73 vs ScanNet++ 0.42) suggest the oracle saving is real.
- *Novelty:* adaptive stopping in 3D exists only for stationary solvers (Solv3R) and DEQ stereo. Allocating compute jointly between loop steps and TTO iterations per scene is open.

**5. (Lower priority) Solv3R + fixed-point acceleration / warm starts from a TTO solution.**
- *Mechanism:* if Solv3R's weights are released, apply Anderson/Broyden acceleration to its calls (no training). Or warm-start its state for a new view set from an existing solution.
- *Cheapest first experiment:* Anderson acceleration with memory 3–5 on Solv3R's calls on the dev subset, then count calls to r_k < 0.02.
- *Caveat:* Solv3R uses *more* calls than DéjàView at K = 12, so this is about anytime convergence, not fewer steps. It depends on an external release.

Other variants worth a footnote:
- Retrofit RLT-style remaining-time feedback (η_k = 1/(K−k)) as part of combination 1's fine-tune, since RLT reports the largest fewer-loop gains in LMs.
- Use Garcia et al.'s lesson: add the true task loss on whatever GT exists, on top of teacher matching.

A possible SIGGRAPH framing: "Loop or optimise? Allocating compute between learned refinement steps and test-time optimisation". It would combine an elastic DéjàView distilled down to K ≈ 3–6 (combinations 1 and 2) with a per-scene (K, T) policy (combination 4) and optional guidance (combination 3), evaluated on the matched-compute plots the project already has.

### Gaps
- The compute estimates for combination 1 (steps per 6 h, cache size) are my back-of-envelope numbers, not measurements.
- It is unknown whether DéjàView's training data overlaps the unlabeled sets we would use for self-distillation. That matters less for self-distillation than for GT fine-tuning, but the benchmark scenes must be excluded.
- I didn't find the RAPTOR (Jacobs et al. 2026) arXiv id; it is cited here only through DéjàView.
- The TTO-on-DéjàView results at K = 3–12 (needed for combinations 2 and 4) were still pending on 2026-11-10 according to the knowledge base.
