# Hybrids of learning and optimisation in multi-view 3D reconstruction (TTO-aware training, amortised optimisation, test-time training, optimiser-ready predictions)

Scope: (1) training feed-forward models so that test-time optimisation (TTO) works better, (2) distilling optimisation/TTO outputs back into networks, (3) test-time training (weight-space adaptation) of 3D foundation models, (4) predicting quantities an optimiser consumes (features, confidences, priors, depth bases). Written 2026-11-15 for a 2-person team whose TTO is a photometric / feature-metric BA on DA3 (SMALL to GIANT) and DéjàView outputs (0.2-0.3 s per scene). Project facts are cited from the repository knowledge base (local files); everything else from papers.

Project baseline used for comparisons below (from the knowledge base): on the 22-scene dev set, DA3-SMALL alone 16.2 AUC@3 at 0.40 s, + photometric PBA T=25 43.8 at 0.71 s total; held-out SMALL 13.6 → 57.2 with the NCC configuration at 4.8 s TTO; LARGE-1.1 48.6 → 71.0; DéjàView K=12 58.7 AUC@3 at 0.96 s, collapsing below K=6 — [knowledge/README.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/README.md), [knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/dejaview-steps.md). Diagnosed: DA3's pose error lies in the weakest directions of the photometric objective (condition number ~6e4; 42-76 % of DA3-SMALL's pose error in the weakest 10 % of eigen-directions, after PBA 60-90 % of the remaining error is there) — [knowledge/ideas/pose-prior-and-flat-directions.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/pose-prior-and-flat-directions.md).

## Q1. Differentiable optimisation layers inside reconstruction networks: how they are trained and what it costs

### Takeaway
Putting a (Gauss-Newton / LM) BA layer inside the network and supervising after every unrolled iteration is the established recipe (BA-Net, DeepV2D, DROID-SLAM, PixLoc, VGGSfM); it is consistently better than training without the layer and bolting BA on at test time, but it is expensive (DROID-SLAM: 1 week on 4 RTX 3090; VGGSfM: 32 A100s over ~7 days of staged training), so a small team should only differentiate through a cheap, small optimiser (their own PBA's final LM step) or through a few heads, not train a backbone with BA in the loop. In 2026 the trend splits into "latent BA" (BA-T, Solv3R: learned iterative operators trained per-iteration) and "explicit BA after a feed-forward model" (BLASt3R, VGGT+BA, Selfi).

### Cited Findings
- BA-Net optimises a feature-metric (photometric-in-feature-space) BA over a small number of coefficients that linearly combine a predicted depth basis, rather than per-pixel depth — as characterised by [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869) (original: [BA-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.04807)).
- DeepV2D alternates between depth updates and camera-pose updates instead of joint bundle adjustment — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869) (original: [DeepV2D](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.04605)).
- DeepFactors (building on CodeSLAM) jointly optimises pose and the parameters of a learned depth basis at inference — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869).
- DROID-SLAM: a ConvGRU predicts flow revisions plus per-pixel confidence weights w_ij and a pixel-wise damping factor λ; a Dense Bundle Adjustment layer (Gauss-Newton with Schur complement) maps them to pose and per-pixel inverse-depth updates and is part of the computation graph; pose and flow losses are applied to the output of every iteration with exponentially increasing weight (γ = 0.9); the first two poses are fixed to ground truth during training to remove gauge freedom, which otherwise "poorly impacts the conditioning of the linear system and the stability of the gradients" — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869).
- DROID-SLAM training cost: TartanAir only, 250k steps, batch 4, 384×512, 7-frame clips, 15 unrolled update iterations, "1 week on 4 RTX-3090 GPUs" — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869).
- DROID-SLAM ablation: training on flow and applying BA only at inference ("RAFT + BA") makes the system "unstable and prone to failure"; the DBA layer must be used during training — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869).
- VGGSfM: tracker → transformer camera initialiser → transformer triangulator → differentiable BA with the Theseus second-order LM solver, which uses the implicit function theorem to backpropagate through the nested optimisation; during training 256 query points and 5 BA steps — [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563).
- VGGSfM cost: 32 NVIDIA A100 (80 GB); Kubric tracker pretraining ~1 day, then tracker 2 days, camera initialiser 2 days, triangulator 1 day, final joint end-to-end training 1 day; at inference each BA step takes ~0.7 s on an A100 (25 frames) — [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563).
- VGGSfM: end-to-end joint training (gradients through BA to tracker and initialisers) raises Co3D AUC@30 70.7 → 74.0 and IMC AUC@10 68.35 → 73.92 versus training components in isolation — [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563).
- Theseus is a library for differentiable nonlinear least squares used for such layers (supports implicit differentiation among other backward modes) — [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563); library paper: [Theseus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09442).
- PixLoc unrolls LM on deep features, supervises only the pose at each pyramid level, learns per-pixel uncertainty weights and a per-parameter, per-level damping λ ∈ R^6; ablation (AUC at 25 cm / 1 m): photometric alignment 1.2/3.3 → deep features trained with the Gauss-Newton loss 13.2/21.4 → unrolled optimiser 49.2/67.0 → + learned confidence 53.3/72.5 → + learned damping 59.8/79.0 — [PixLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09213).
- PixLoc argues networks should predict only features/weights and leave geometry to the optimiser, and deliberately avoids networks that predict the robust cost, damping or update from content because that "can greatly impair the ability to generalize" — [PixLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09213).
- BA-T (TUM/ETH, NeurIPS 2026) implements BA-style camera/geometry updates as one reusable transformer layer in latent token space (latent residual → camera update by cross-attention ≈ Schur step → geometry update); supervised at every iteration with weights α^(K−k); 38M-parameter decoder; trained 2 days on 8 H100 — [BA-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287).
- BA-T ablation: applying the decoder K times but supervising only the last iteration lowers rotation/translation accuracy ("supervising every iteration is what makes the refinement progressive"); iterative training of a plain decoder helps but leaves a large gap to the BA-structured layer — [BA-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287).
- BA-T vs classical BA from the same initialisation (two views, 7-Scenes, DROID-SLAM's GN BA with GT intrinsics): rotation AUC@5 0.53 (DROID BA) vs 0.68 (BA-T), δ<1.25 0.36 vs 0.98, 161 ms vs 31 ms — [BA-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287).
- Solv3R (2026-11-02) fine-tunes DéjàView's looped block into a stationary (iteration-index-free) "solver"; trained by running 16-40 no-gradient calls, then 8 differentiable calls supervised at the final call, with 15 % probability of perturbing the state (Gaussian noise or resetting views) and distillation from DéjàView's 16-step output; ~1 week on 8×H100 — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- Solv3R shows DéjàView loses accuracy beyond its trained step range (AUC@5 76.5 at 16 calls, 0.0 at 128 and 1024 calls) while the stationary solver keeps 79.1/78.8; an adaptive stop on relative latent update (< 0.02) needs ~17.5 calls on average and raises ETH3D AUC@3 from 66.0 (DéjàView) to 70.4 — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- BLASt3R (NAVER, 2026-09) pairs a multi-view matcher with a monocular network predicting "adjustable pointmaps" (a main log-depth channel plus corrective channels combined linearly by BA) and solves a unified GPU LM BA over cameras, tracks and the low-dimensional depth coefficients; the networks are trained with ordinary supervised losses, not through the BA — [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210).
- MegaSaM and ViPE (optimisation-based, DROID-style with dense depth priors) "remain computationally expensive and require careful system design" according to [PURE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14698); MegaSaM itself: [MegaSaM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04463).
- Diff3R reports that, like VGGSfM's differentiable BA, training through the optimiser increases per-step training time 4-5× relative to plain feed-forward training, with no test-time overhead — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).

### Inferences
- Every successful "optimiser-in-the-loop" system co-designs the network with the optimiser from (near) scratch on large data (TartanAir, MegaDepth, CO3D, 6-dataset mixtures) at 4-32 GPU-weeks. That is out of reach; the transferable lessons are (a) supervise after each iteration, (b) let the network output weights/damping/features, not updates, (c) remove gauge freedom when backpropagating through BA, (d) implicit differentiation avoids unrolling memory.
- Their PBA already forms the Gauss-Newton normal equations for poses, one focal, an 8-cell log-depth grid and a radial term analytically. Implicit differentiation through the final LM solution therefore costs one extra linear solve with a matrix they already build, which makes "differentiate only through our own small optimiser into small heads" cheap (see Q5/Q6).
- Solv3R's finding that DéjàView is step-conditioned and collapses outside its schedule is directly relevant to running DéjàView at K = 4-6 and then PBA: the intermediate K states were never trained to be decoded as final outputs except via the step conditioning; this partially explains the steep collapse below K = 6 recorded in the knowledge base.

### Gaps
- No training-compute numbers were verified for DPVO, DeepV2D or BA-Net in this session.
- No paper was found that trains a large pretrained multi-view transformer (DA3, VGGT, π³, DéjàView) end-to-end through an explicit dense photometric BA layer; whether the DROID-style "train with DBA" benefit transfers to fine-tuning a pretrained model is unknown.

## Q2. Learned features, confidences and uncertainties for feature-metric refinement; can a small head on DA3/DéjàView produce TTO-ready features or weights cheaply?

### Takeaway
There is a mature line of work showing that features trained *for* an optimiser (GN-Net's Gauss-Newton loss, PixLoc's unrolled LM, D3VO's predicted photometric uncertainty and brightness, DROID's confidence/damping) beat generic features by large margins, and 2025-2026 work shows that a small adapter on a frozen feed-forward 3D model can be trained self-supervised from the model's own outputs to give BA-usable features (Selfi on VGGT). A frozen-DA3 feature head trained with a GN-style or unrolled-PBA loss is therefore plausible and cheap; the project's negative DINO/DA3-token result used raw PCA tokens, not trained ones. Separately, feed-forward models' internal features predict their own pose failures far better than their confidence outputs (linear probe ROC-AUC 93.5 % vs 79.7 %).

### Cited Findings
- Pixel-Perfect SfM refines tracks and structure from COLMAP through feature-metric keypoint adjustment and feature-metric BA on dense CNN features — summarised by [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563) (original: [PixSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08291)).
- GN-Net (TUM) trains a Siamese U-Net with a pixelwise contrastive loss plus a "Gauss-Newton loss": for a correspondence, start at a random point in a vicinity, form H = JᵀJ and b from the feature residual, and minimise the negative log-likelihood of the true match under the Gaussian N(x_s − H⁻¹b, H⁻¹) — which rewards both a correct GN step and a calibrated Hessian, enforcing "a large basin of convergence for the subsequent Gauss-Newton optimization"; trained from correspondences produced by Stereo DSO depths/poses, no human labels — [GN-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11932).
- GN-Net argues training on per-pixel GN problems (2×2 systems) transfers to 6-DoF pose because pose Jacobians are smooth, and gives over a thousand supervision signals per image pair instead of one; it beats DSO, ORB-SLAM, D2-Net and SuperPoint on cross-weather relocalisation, and the GN loss clearly beats the contrastive loss alone; on EuRoC it improves DSO by almost a factor of 2 at low frame rates — [GN-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11932).
- PixLoc's learned per-pixel uncertainty is not supervised; it is learned to maximise pose accuracy and down-weights dynamic objects and repeated patterns that create local minima — [PixLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09213).
- PixLoc tried training its CNN with the GN loss of GN-Net and reports the unrolled formulation works much better in its wide-baseline setting (13.2 vs 49.2 AUC@25 cm in the ablation) — [PixLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09213).
- D3VO predicts depth, pose, and photometric uncertainty (and is integrated with DSO, i.e. a photometric BA back-end) — listed in DROID-SLAM's EuRoC comparison as "D3VO + DSO" — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869); original: [D3VO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01060).
- Selfi freezes VGGT, adds a DPT feature adapter (24-channel per-pixel features) on four intermediate token maps, and trains it with a reprojection-based soft-argmax correspondence loss whose targets come from VGGT's own predicted depth and cameras (pseudo-GT, visibility-filtered); the features are then matched by dot products and used in BA — [Selfi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08930).
- Selfi BA results: DL3DV AUC@3 0.813 → 0.870 with BA; Tanks&Temples 0.818 → 0.955; on 100 frames VGGT 0.845 → 0.876, where VGGT's own CoTracker-based BA runs out of memory — [Selfi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08930).
- Selfi notes the features improve poses via BA "despite training on pseudo-GT with zero projection error", and that a CLIP-style contrastive loss collapsed its features (all queries matched one point) — [Selfi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08930).
- DROID-SLAM's network predicts per-pixel confidence weights used as the Mahalanobis weights of the BA cost and a per-pixel damping factor for the depth block — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869).
- VGGSfM's tracker predicts an aleatoric variance per track point (trained by Gaussian negative log-likelihood); its inverse is used as confidence to filter BA terms — [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563).
- Diff3R predicts per-Gaussian, per-attribute proximal weights Λ ("uncertainty") with a small MLP on DA3/DepthSplat DPT features; Λ is learned purely from the outer novel-view loss through implicit gradients (∇_Λ = −v ⊙ (Θ* − Θ0)) without uncertainty labels, and learns to anchor regions without multi-view constraints — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- "Does VGGT Know What It Does Not Know?": a linear probe (logistic regression on 128 PCA dims of mean-pooled tokens) on frozen VGGT features predicts pose failure on held-out HardMatch categories with ROC-AUC 93.5 % vs 79.7 % for VGGT's point-map confidence and 77.7 % for a classifier on all outputs; the signal is absent in the per-image encoder and appears abruptly in the middle aggregator blocks; same for π³ (91.7 %) — [Thirumani Srinivasan & Nordström 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35806).
- The same probe transfers to rotation failures on PhotoTourism (AUC 94.6-97.5 %) but not to short-baseline translation failures on ScanNet-1500 (44-48 %), for which a probe trained on ScanNet reaches 90.5-91 %; filtering rotation-averaging edges by the probe gave no downstream gain because failures were < 2 % of edges — [Thirumani Srinivasan & Nordström 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35806).
- VGGT's indoor failures are translation-direction failures concentrated at short baselines (47 % of pairs with < 10 cm baseline have > 15° translation-direction error) — [Thirumani Srinivasan & Nordström 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35806).
- BLASt3R's monocular network predicts m depth channels (main + corrective) trained so that the best per-image linear combination fits GT log-depth (IRLS for an ℓ1 fit, gradients stopped through α, β); using 16 channels instead of 1 improves pointmap accuracy after BA by a "consistent 30-40 %", and the corrective channels concentrate on local regions and depth edges — [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210).
- Project result: DA3 backbone tokens (PCA-reduced, not trained) as a coarse PBA level were neutral on DA3 initialisations (they only widen the basin at 15° perturbations), and token mutual-nearest-neighbour matches were wrong (only 6-32 % within one 14-px token, positional bias) — [knowledge/ideas/dino-features.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/dino-features.md).
- Project result: VGG16 relu1_2+relu2_2, PCA to 8 channels, gives the best F1 on LARGE/GIANT; VGG + Huber costs 2.22 ms per Adam iteration vs 1.24 ms for grey Huber and needs 10.4 GiB peak memory at 100 frames (does not fit the 8 GB local GPUs) — [knowledge/ideas/feature-metric-vgg.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/feature-metric-vgg.md).
- Project result: DA3 depth confidence separates DA3-SMALL's gross-failure frames with AUROC ~0.90; the reference view decides which frames fail; an oracle fix of gross frames before PBA is worth at most +1.5 AUC@3 / +4.1 AUC@30 — [knowledge/ideas/gross-failures-and-reference-view.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/gross-failures-and-reference-view.md).

### Inferences
- A head on frozen DA3 intermediate tokens (DA3 already computes them; the patched DA3 keeps 4 output layers) that outputs ~8 channels at 1/2-1/4 resolution could replace the separate VGG forward pass (saving its memory and ~1 ms/iteration) and be trained specifically for the PBA objective. Training signals in order of cost: (1) GN-Net loss on correspondences obtained from GT depth+poses (ScanNet++/HiRoom train) or from DA3+long-PBA pseudo-labels; (2) Selfi-style soft-argmax reprojection loss with DA3's own outputs as targets (fully self-supervised, unlimited data); (3) PixLoc-style unrolling of 3-5 LM steps of their PBA with a pose loss (most faithful, most expensive).
- The GN-loss is attractive for this project because it rewards a correct Hessian, i.e. it trains features whose curvature reflects true uncertainty — relevant to the diagnosed weak directions, where the current objective is flat.
- The probe paper suggests per-frame "is this frame grossly wrong" and possibly per-frame expected post-PBA error are linearly readable from DA3/DéjàView tokens; given the oracle ceiling of +1.5 AUC@3 for fixing gross frames, the payoff is more plausibly in setting per-frame prior strengths (where the knowledge base shows priors help good inits and hurt bad ones) than in relocalisation.
- BLASt3R's adjustable depth channels are a learned alternative to the project's fixed 8-cell log-depth grid: a network can predict *which* low-dimensional depth corrections are plausible, instead of a spatial grid that can also "explain away" pose errors.

### Gaps
- No paper found that trains a feature head on DA3 or DéjàView specifically for dense photometric/feature-metric BA, or that compares such a head against VGG features at matched time.
- Whether DA3-SMALL tokens (ViT-S, patch 14) carry enough sub-pixel information for fine PBA levels is untested; Selfi used VGGT (1B) tokens.
- No verified numbers on D3VO's exact gains or on FeatDepth-style "convergent" feature losses were retrieved in this session.

## Q3. Amortised optimisation and self-distillation: networks trained on optimised outputs (teacher = model + TTO, student = model)

### Takeaway
2026 saw a burst of label-free self-improvement for 3D foundation models, but almost all of it uses *the model itself with more context* as teacher (SelfEvo, Free Geometry) or *external 2D priors/matches* (PURE, Self-Geometry); only LoRA3D (per scene, DUSt3R) and, loosely, Selfi use the model's *optimised* outputs as pseudo-labels. Dataset-level distillation of a cheap photometric BA into DA3/DéjàView, studied under matched compute, is not covered by anything found. A key caveat for this project: self-distilling from PBA cannot teach the weak directions PBA itself does not fix; a stronger teacher (larger model + TTO, or GT) is needed for those.

### Cited Findings
- LoRA3D (NVIDIA): DUSt3R's pairwise predictions are aligned by a robust global optimisation in which confidences become optimisable weights updated in closed form (Geman-McClure-style IRLS, w = C/(1+‖e‖/μ)²); high-calibrated-confidence refined points become pseudo-labels; DUSt3R is LoRA-fine-tuned (rank 16 on all attention weights) with its pretraining loss — [LoRA3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07746).
- LoRA3D cost/benefit: 10 calibration images, fine-tuning converges in < 3.5 min (batch 2, peak < 20 GB), whole self-calibration < 5 min on one GPU (RTX 3090), adapter < 18 MB; up to 88 % improvement over 161 scenes (Replica, TUM, Waymo); pseudo-labelling with uncalibrated confidence hurts; performance saturates around 10 calibration images; gains for MASt3R were "mostly marginal" — [LoRA3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07746).
- SelfEvo: online self-distillation with context asymmetry — teacher (EMA of student, λ = 0.995 for VGGT) sees 24-64 frames, student a random 2-12-frame subset, losses are the model's own training losses with teacher outputs as targets; frame dropping beats photometric or crop asymmetry; online EMA teacher beats offline pseudo-label fine-tuning; freezing the camera decoder is best (camera pseudo-supervision is distorted by mismatched frame subsets) — [SelfEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08532).
- SelfEvo results: e.g. VGGT on DROID camera AUC@5 77.22 → 86.48; π³ 77.58 → 81.06; up to 36.5 % relative depth improvement and 20.1 % camera improvement; trained 20 epochs × 50 steps; original-domain benchmarks preserved — [SelfEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08532).
- Free Geometry: teacher = frozen model on 8 views, student = LoRA model on the 4 even-indexed views; loss on backbone features before the per-view decoders (intra-frame consistency + cross-frame relational/triangle terms); adaptation per *dataset*, ~2 min per dataset on an RTX Pro 6000; average +3.73 % relative pose accuracy, +2.88 % pointmap; DA3-Giant ETH3D AUC@3 (100 views) 0.485 → 0.522 — [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048).
- Free Geometry observes that in DA3 and VGGT all cross-view reasoning happens in the backbone while decoders are per view, motivating feature-level supervision; LoRA rank 32 best, rank 64 worse — [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048).
- PURE (ACM MM 2026): post-training without GT using MoGe depth (ordinal + local affine-invariant losses) and GIM-RoMa correspondences for pose refinement; 4×H800, DA3 8 h (5k iterations), VGGT 20 h (12k); data from 1.8k SpatialVID + 4k Sekai scenes plus synthetic; gains saturate with data scale (relative AbsRel 0.85 at 0.2M images, 0.75 at 1M, 0.72 at 5M); post-training on the test sets themselves helps less than large unlabeled corpora — [PURE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14698).
- Selfi uses VGGT's own depth/cameras as pseudo-GT to train a feature adapter (distilling the model's outputs into an aligned feature space), then runs BA on matches from those features — [Selfi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08930).
- Light3R-SfM amortises global optimisation: a learnable latent global-alignment attention module replaces DUSt3R/MASt3R-style global optimisation; 200 images in 33 s vs ~27 min for MASt3R-SfM (> 49×); trained 100k iterations on 8 A100-80GB; accuracy competitive at loose thresholds but a gap remains at tight thresholds, and the authors suggest "a small optimization stage on top of the regressed outputs" — [Light3R-SfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14914).
- Solv3R distils DéjàView's 16-step predictions into its solver (λ_dist = 0.5) and uses distillation-only supervision on datasets with sparse/noisy GT — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- OTT3R distils π³ (959M) into a 102M student using an offline cache of teacher pseudo-labels, framed as "1 % compute" — [OTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36374) (abstract-level; details from project CLAUDE.md).
- Amortised optimisation as a general framework (learning a model that predicts the solution of an optimisation problem, optionally followed by refinement) is surveyed in [Amos, Tutorial on amortized optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00665).
- Project history: earlier distillation/fine-tuning runs (10 ScanNet++ train scenes, 600-1000 batch-1 steps) were limited by data and optimisation; students trained on 8-16-view subsets improved on 32-frame validation but not on the 100-frame benchmark; DA3 fine-tuning needs small learning rates (5e-6 backbone, 2e-5 heads) — [knowledge/ideas/distillation.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/distillation.md).
- Project history: re-running DA3 conditioned on PBA-refined cameras gave no consistent gain after the hybrid optimiser (F1 down 0.4-6, DTU Chamfer better) — [knowledge/ideas/da3-reconditioning.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/da3-reconditioning.md).

### Inferences
- Because their PBA costs ~0.3 s per scene on top of a 0.4 s DA3-SMALL forward, a pseudo-label set of several thousand unlabeled scenes is cheap to produce (my estimate: ~1 h of one RTX 5090 per few thousand scenes, from the knowledge-base timings), which removes the data limit that sank the earlier GT-based fine-tuning. LoRA3D and PURE show that optimisation-refined or correspondence-constrained pseudo-labels improve 3D models; SelfEvo shows online EMA teachers beat offline caches.
- PBA output = DA3 output corrected in strong directions; its residual error is concentrated in weak directions. A student trained on PBA outputs learns the strong-direction corrections (which PBA would make anyway at test time for 0.3 s) and inherits the weak-direction errors. At matched compute this is likely to help the student *alone* (better feed-forward) but help student + PBA little. Testing exactly this is a clean, publishable negative-or-positive result; the more promising variant uses a stronger teacher (GIANT + PBA reaches 74.9-77.2 dev AUC@3 vs SMALL + PBA 43.8-55.6) and restricts the loss to the weak subspace (Q6, idea 1).
- For DéjàView, "teacher = K=12 (+PBA), student = K=4/6" is the natural amortisation of the loop steps; Solv3R shows distilling DéjàView's own 16-step output while changing the loop dynamics is viable, but at 1 week × 8 H100 scale.

### Gaps
- No paper found that distils a dense photometric BA into a feed-forward multi-view model at dataset scale, or that reports student + TTO vs teacher + TTO at matched compute.
- No published evidence on whether self-distillation from an optimiser transfers improvement in weak (ill-conditioned) directions; the inference above is reasoning, not a measured result.
- Examples in optical flow (SMURF-style self-teaching) and monocular depth (Depth Anything's pseudo-label self-training is mentioned in [SelfEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08532)) were not examined in detail.

## Q4. Test-time training (weight-space adaptation) of 3D foundation models, its cost, and how it compares with output-space TTO

### Takeaway
Weight-space TTA for DUSt3R/VGGT/π³/DA3 is now crowded (Test3R, TTT3R, LoRA3D, TCO, Free Geometry, Self-Geometry, Online3R, Info3R, 3R-Adapter, CAPA, ...), costs 30 s to several minutes per scene, and on the DA3 benchmark gives small gains: the best published DA3 numbers (Self-Geometry) move DA3-SMALL mean AUC@3 from 0.10 to 0.12 at 0.3-2.5 min per scene, whereas the project's output-space PBA moves DA3-SMALL from 16.2 to 43.8 dev AUC@3 in 0.3 s. Output-space TTO dominates at matched compute by orders of magnitude; weight-space adaptation is better viewed as a per-dataset/domain step (Free Geometry adapts per dataset) that could be stacked under PBA.

### Cited Findings
- Test3R: for triplets (I1, I2, I3), minimise ‖X¹_ref − X²_ref‖ between I1's pointmaps from pairs (I1, I2) and (I1, I3) by tuning visual prompts (32 tokens per encoder layer, 0.79M parameters) of DUSt3R; 1 epoch over ≤ 165 sampled triplets; ~30 s TTT + ~10 s inference per scene on an RTX 4090; DTU multi-view depth rel 3.3 → 2.0, inlier ratio 69.9 → 84.1; 7-Scenes accuracy 0.146 → 0.105 — [Test3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13750).
- TTT3R reinterprets CUT3R's recurrent state update as test-time training with fast weights and derives a closed-form, training-free per-token learning rate from state-observation attention confidence; ~2× better global pose on long sequences at 20 FPS and 6 GB; fine-tuning with the rule gives only marginal pose gains (because training sequences are ≤ 64 frames) — [TTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26645).
- TTT3R notes that CVD and Test3R "fine-tune a pre-trained model on the test sequence to minimize a self-supervised geometric consistency loss" and lists Robust-CVD and CasualSAM as per-sequence methods that jointly optimise cameras and dense depth — [TTT3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26645) (CVD: [Luo et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.15021)).
- FlowMap optimises a depth network per scene from offline optical flow and point tracks in an end-to-end differentiable SfM pipeline — described in [Light3R-SfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14914); it struggles with discontinuous/low-overlap image sets — [LoRA3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07746).
- TCO ("Learning 3D Reconstruction with Priors in Test Time"): LoRA (rank 4) on the shared decoder of VGGT/π³ with heads frozen, 5-80 iterations (40 default), loss = 2DGS-rendered photometric (or geometric) compatibility between views + penalties to known priors (poses, intrinsics, depth); ETH3D VGGT accuracy 0.280 → 0.270 with the compatibility loss alone and → 0.114 with pose+intrinsics priors; fine-tuning the heads hurts; directly optimising 2DGS parameters with VGGT frozen is much worse (0.895); inference time is acknowledged as a limitation — [TCO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03878).
- Self-Geometry: per-scene LoRA (rank 64 on QKV) TTA with LightGlue correspondences as pseudo-GT; a point-to-point reprojection loss (pose + depth) and a depth-independent epipolar loss (pose only), with the reprojection gradient projected orthogonal to the epipolar gradient (they conflict in 42.4 % of iterations); 50 AdamW iterations; evaluated on the DA3 benchmark protocol (7-Scenes, ETH3D, ScanNet++, HiRoom; DTU excluded) — [Self-Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10708).
- Self-Geometry mean AUC@3 (4 datasets): DA3-S 0.10 → 0.12, DA3-B 0.19 → 0.22, DA3-L 0.44 → 0.45, DA3-G 0.60 → 0.61; most of the DA3-S/B gain is HiRoom (DA3-S 0.09 → 0.16); unposed F1 mean DA3-S 0.32 → 0.36 — [Self-Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10708).
- Self-Geometry cost per scene on an RTX PRO 6000: DA3-Small 0.49 min (ETH3D), 2.46 (7-Scenes), 2.19 (ScanNet++), 0.33 (HiRoom); DA3-Giant 1.65/3.99/3.94/1.84 min; LightGlue matching alone ~0.26 min — [Self-Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10708).
- Self-Geometry's comparison: Free Geometry gives mean AUC@3 +4.0 % (DA3-S) to +2.5 % (DA3-L) relative; TCO with the model's own poses as prior collapses on π³ (mean AUC@3 −91.5 %) — [Self-Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10708).
- Free Geometry adapts once per dataset (~2 min per dataset), and improvements transfer across view counts (trained 8→4, gains at 4-100 views) — [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048).
- LoRA3D: ~5 min per scene (one GPU), up to 88 % improvement on DUSt3R — [LoRA3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07746).
- CAPA adapts pre-trained 3D foundation models for depth completion with parameter-efficient test-time optimisation driven by sparse geometric cues (abstract-level) — [CAPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14751).
- Other 2026 TTA/online-adaptation entries found but not read in detail: [Online3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09480), [Info3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21938), [3R-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11964), [HIST3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12011), [BAT3R (Bayesian state updates)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16330), [Rethinking Asymmetry in Self-Supervised 3D Geometry Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42092).
- Project: DA3 depth-head fine-tuning per scene inside PBA (Test3R-like, cameras free) gave the same pose and worse recon on SMALL at 2× time, better recon on LARGE (7-Scenes F1 59.9 → 63.6, ScanNet++ 61.4 → 66.4) at 8× time (38.6 vs 4.7 s) — [knowledge/ideas/depth-parametrisation.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/depth-parametrisation.md).

### Inferences
- Caveat on the comparison: Self-Geometry's DA3 baselines (mean of 4 datasets, no DTU) and the project's dev/held-out sets differ, so absolute numbers are not directly comparable; but the magnitudes (+2 AUC@3 points in minutes vs +28 points in 0.3 s for DA3-SMALL) leave no doubt that output-space TTO is far more compute-efficient for pose. This is itself a citable positioning statement for the paper: "weight-space TTA vs output-space BA at matched compute" has not been plotted by anyone.
- Weight-space methods change all outputs coherently (depth and cameras through the network), which output-space PBA with an 8-cell grid cannot; their best use is likely recon F1 on large models (consistent with the project's LARGE depth-head result) or as a one-off per-dataset adaptation (Free Geometry) that is amortised over many scenes and then followed by PBA.
- Self-Geometry's gradient-conflict finding (reprojection vs epipolar) mirrors the project's finding that correspondence terms inside PBA lower AUC; the epipolar (depth-independent) term is a candidate for the "wide-baseline term for weak modes" listed as open in the knowledge base.

### Gaps
- No paper reports TTA on DéjàView, or TTA followed by output-space BA.
- Timings for TCO were not retrieved (its table of inference times was not in the returned pages).
- Info3R, Online3R, 3R-Adapter details unknown beyond titles/abstract snippets.

## Q5. Training predictions to land in the basin of TTO (bilevel / implicit differentiation, optimisation-aware training, predicting in well-conditioned directions)

### Takeaway
The clearest modern example is Diff3R (TUM, 2026): a feed-forward 3DGS model (including a DA3-based pose-free variant) is trained through a 50-300-step TTO via the implicit function theorem with a Gauss-Newton Hessian and matrix-free PCG, plus learned per-parameter proximal weights; the network learns a worse zero-shot but better post-optimisation initialisation. Classical precedents are PixLoc/GN-Net (features for a basin), DROID/VGGSfM (train through BA), EPro-PnP and DSAC (train through solvers). Nothing found does this for camera/depth BA on top of DA3/VGGT/DéjàView, and nothing explicitly trains a predictor to be accurate only in the optimiser's weak directions — an open gap that maps exactly onto the project's diagnosis.

### Cited Findings
- Diff3R formulates bilevel optimisation: inner loss = rendering residual + proximal term (Θ − Θ0)ᵀ diag(Λ) (Θ − Θ0); outer loss = novel-view rendering after the inner TTO; gradient ∂Θ*/∂Θ0 = Λ(H + Λ)⁻¹ via the implicit function theorem with H ≈ JᵀJ, solved matrix-free with PCG; ∇_Θ0 = Λ ⊙ v and ∇_Λ = −v ⊙ (Θ* − Θ0) reuse the same solve — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- Diff3R notes the backward linear system is "structurally identical to the normal equations solved during a Levenberg-Marquardt step", with the proximal weight acting as LM damping; with λ → 0 the gradient to the initialisation vanishes (the result no longer depends on the start), with large λ it passes straight through — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- Diff3R results: pose-free DA3 on RE10K TTO gain +0.76 dB vs +0.60 dB for vanilla DA3 (22.81 vs 22.58 PSNR after TTO); ScanNet++ DSLR +0.35 dB vs DA3 after TTO; pose-given DepthSplat +0.274 dB vs +0.029 dB; ablations show both the proximal term and learned Λ are needed; two-stage training (feed-forward pretraining, then activate the inner loop and fine-tune only the Gaussian DPT head and uncertainty MLP) — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- Diff3R's qualitative curves show its initial predictions can render worse zero-shot but converge to a better minimum ("optimization-aware initialization") — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- MAML/meta-learning and implicit-MAML are the conceptual ancestors cited by Diff3R; meta-learned initialisations were used for coordinate networks (MetaSDF, meta-learned NeRF) — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- GN-Net's Gauss-Newton loss trains representations "designed to admit a large basin of convergence for the subsequent Gauss-Newton optimization" — [GN-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11932).
- PixLoc trains features and confidences "to maximize the pose accuracy" after unrolled LM, learning weights that suppress features creating local minima — [PixLoc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09213).
- DROID-SLAM supervises poses after each BA iteration and fixes gauge freedom during training; training without the BA layer fails at test time — [DROID-SLAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10869).
- VGGSfM supervises both the initial (pre-BA) and BA-refined cameras and points, so the initialisers learn to feed BA — [VGGSfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04563).
- PoseDiffusion uses geometry-guided sampling that enforces epipolar constraints across views during diffusion sampling of cameras — [LoRA3D's description](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07746) (original: [PoseDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15667)).
- Learned refiners that act like optimisers on feed-forward outputs: PosePlaner denoises any initial relative pose with a one-step flow-matching transformer conditioned on plane-warping residuals of matched planar primitives, fusing per-match hypotheses with a score head and recovering translation by a regularised LM; 128 GPU-days on A800; can regress on already-accurate inits — [PosePlaner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845).
- PosePlaner's translation solver adapts its Tikhonov prior to the initialisation's self-consistency (large prior when the init already agrees with the plane field, small when it does not) — [PosePlaner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845).
- Weak directions in classical BA: CSS-BA restricts each Schur-LM step to a low-dimensional subspace built from geometrically gated, high-gain camera blocks (Lanczos/Ritz basis) without changing the objective; on near-rotational PhoneSweep AUC@30 rises from 12-13 (standard LM) and 33-43 (PoBA) to 80-84, at ~2× the runtime of standard LM; on well-conditioned BAL problems it is comparable, not better — [CSS-BA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15652).
- CSS-BA explains that small baselines entangle translation, depth and focal length into near-null camera-increment directions after Schur elimination, so LM can reach small reprojection error with inaccurate relative poses — [CSS-BA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15652).
- EPro-PnP trains 2D-3D correspondences and weights end-to-end through a probabilistic PnP layer (pose distribution + KL loss) — [EPro-PnP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.13254) (known paper; details not re-read in this session).
- Project: pose priors toward DA3 (w_pose = 10) recovered ETH3D/7-Scenes for good inits (LARGE dev 56.7 → 60.2) but did not pay for SMALL (31.4 → 30.8); a pose basis from covisibility-Laplacian eigenvectors overfit dev (held-out +0.2 to +0.5 only) — [knowledge/ideas/pose-prior-and-flat-directions.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/pose-prior-and-flat-directions.md).

### Inferences
- The IFT gradient Λ(H + Λ)⁻¹ is a projection that is ~0 along directions with large curvature (where the optimiser fixes the error regardless of the init) and ~1 along flat directions (where the result stays at the init). Training DA3/DéjàView heads with the post-PBA loss through this gradient therefore automatically focuses the network's correction on the weak subspace — precisely "predict in the weak directions, leave strong directions to optimisation". This is a principled, testable story for the project and appears unexplored for camera BA.
- A first-order, solver-free approximation: run PBA once (non-differentiably), take the GN Hessian H at the solution (already formed in LM), and train with a loss on the network's pose error weighted by Λ(H + Λ)⁻¹ (or projected on H's smallest eigenvectors). This needs GT or a teacher that is accurate in those directions.
- Diff3R learns Λ per parameter from the outer loss; the analogue here is per-frame (or per-frame × 6-DoF) prior weights for the PBA pose prior, i.e. learning when to trust DA3 — which the knowledge base shows matters (helps LARGE, hurts SMALL) but was only tuned globally.

### Gaps
- No work found that trains a feed-forward multi-view geometry model through a camera/depth BA with implicit differentiation, nor one that explicitly targets the optimiser's weak subspace in the training loss.
- IFT assumes the inner optimisation has converged; the project's PBA uses Adam on blurred levels then a few LM iterations with a stop rule. How well the IFT gradient at a non-converged LM point approximates the true sensitivity is unknown for this setup.
- No measured evidence on whether a predictor can learn weak-direction corrections from images at all (the probe paper shows short-baseline translation failures are linearly readable in-domain, which is weakly encouraging).

## Q6. Novelty status (late 2026), compute, combination with DéjàView/DA3, gaps a small team can fill, and concrete idea sketches

### Takeaway
Novelty by strand: (3) weight-space TTA of 3D foundation models is saturated; (2) label-free self-distillation is crowded but almost never uses *optimiser outputs* and never studies matched compute; (1) and (4) — training through or for a downstream camera/depth optimiser on top of pretrained multi-view transformers — are essentially open for DA3/DéjàView (Diff3R covers only 3DGS NVS; Selfi only track-BA features; BLASt3R trains its depth basis without the BA). The most distinctive angle for this project is "learn only what the optimiser cannot fix": weak-subspace-aware training and distillation, learned depth bases and prior weights for the PBA, all cheap because they touch heads and reuse the PBA's own Gauss-Newton system.

### Cited Findings
- Diff3R is the only found example of bilevel training of a feed-forward 3D model (including DA3) through test-time optimisation, and it targets novel-view synthesis, not cameras/depth — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- Selfi trains a BA-ready feature adapter on frozen VGGT from its own outputs — [Selfi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08930); BLASt3R predicts BA-adjustable depth channels trained by supervised fitting — [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210).
- Recent TTA/self-improvement papers on DA3 explicitly: Free Geometry (DA3-Giant, VGGT) — [Free Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14048); Self-Geometry (DA3-S/B/L/G, VGGT, π³) — [Self-Geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10708); PURE (DA3, VGGT, π³) — [PURE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14698).
- Solv3R (2026-11-02) already turns DéjàView into a stationary solver with stability/correction training and adaptive stopping, but does not combine it with an explicit optimiser ("without ... a separate optimization backend") — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857).
- BA-T plans to integrate its latent BA layer into multi-view frameworks (VGGT, DA3) as future work — [BA-T](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03287).
- Costs to calibrate expectations: SelfEvo 20 epochs × 50 steps of full post-training — [SelfEvo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08532); PURE 8 h on 4×H800 for DA3 — [PURE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14698); Solv3R ~1 week on 8×H100 — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857); Light3R-SfM 100k iterations on 8 A100 — [Light3R-SfM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14914); PosePlaner 128 A800 GPU-days — [PosePlaner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34845); LoRA3D < 5 min per scene on one 3090 — [LoRA3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07746); Diff3R training 4-5× slower per step than feed-forward — [Diff3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01030).
- Project constraints: RTX 3080 10 GB / RTX 3070 8 GB locally, ~4 SLURM nodes with one RTX 5090 32 GB each, 6 h job limit; earlier fine-tuning on 10 ScanNet++ scenes was data-limited — project CLAUDE.md and [knowledge/ideas/distillation.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/distillation.md).
- Project: 12-16 cell depth grids hold poses better in GT-start tests but the dense LM system does not fit memory at 100 frames; free per-sample depth explains away pose errors from DA3's start (29.6 vs 33.9) — [knowledge/ideas/depth-parametrisation.md](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/depth-parametrisation.md).

### Inferences

**Gaps a small team could fill (ranked by fit to the project):**
1. Weak-subspace-aware training/distillation: train or distil feed-forward predictions only along the optimiser's ill-conditioned directions (derived from the PBA Hessian). Not found anywhere.
2. Optimiser-ready predictions on top of DA3/DéjàView for dense photometric BA: learned corrective depth channels (replacing the 8-cell grid), learned per-frame prior weights, and a feature head replacing VGG — trained through or for the PBA. Partially covered only in other settings (BLASt3R, Diff3R, Selfi).
3. A matched-compute comparison of the three hybrid families on the DA3 benchmark: output-space TTO (theirs), weight-space TTA (Self-Geometry / Free Geometry / Test3R-style LoRA), and distilled students — no paper plots accuracy vs total seconds across these.
4. Distilling a cheap optimiser into a smaller or shorter-loop model (DA3-SMALL, DéjàView K = 4-6) at dataset scale with unlabeled data, and measuring student + TTO vs teacher + TTO.
5. DéjàView/Solv3R + explicit PBA: whether a stationary looped solver can consume or be interleaved with optimiser updates (Solv3R explicitly runs without an optimisation back-end).

**Idea sketches** (compute numbers are my estimates from the knowledge-base timings unless cited):

1. **"Distil only what the optimiser cannot fix" (weak-subspace distillation across model sizes).**
   - Hypothesis: SMALL + PBA lags LARGE/GIANT + PBA mainly because of errors in PBA's weak directions; training SMALL's camera output to match a stronger teacher *only in those directions* closes more of the gap at test time than full distillation, and costs nothing extra at inference.
   - Trained: LoRA on DA3-SMALL's backbone (or only its camera head) and/or a small residual pose head.
   - Data: unlabeled scenes (ScanNet++/HiRoom/7-Scenes train splits, DL3DV, any video); teacher = DA3-GIANT-1.1 (+ PBA, 74.9-77.2 dev AUC@3) or GT where available. For each scene, run SMALL + PBA, take the GN Hessian at its solution, and project the SMALL-vs-teacher pose difference onto the smallest-eigenvalue subspace (or weight by Λ(H+Λ)⁻¹, Q5).
   - Compute: teacher labels ~3-4 s per scene (GIANT forward + PBA) → ~1,000 scenes per 5090-hour; LoRA fine-tuning of DA3-SMALL a few thousand steps in one 6 h job.
   - Cheapest first experiment: no training — on dev, replace SMALL's poses by GIANT's poses *only in the weak subspace* (oracle projection, as with `scripts/weak_modes.py` at commit 1c1a921), run PBA, and measure AUC@3. If the oracle does not approach GIANT + PBA, stop.
   - Risk: weak-subspace errors may not be predictable from SMALL's features (they arise from global ambiguity); teacher errors also live in weak directions; the Hessian depends on the solution (iterate).

2. **Learned corrective depth basis for the PBA (BLASt3R/BA-Net/CodeSLAM on DA3 or DéjàView).**
   - Hypothesis: a few network-predicted corrective log-depth channels per frame (combined linearly by PBA) correct DA3's depth errors better than an 8-cell spatial grid and, being image-adaptive, explain away fewer pose errors than finer grids or free depth.
   - Trained: a light DPT/conv head on frozen DA3 (or DéjàView) features outputting m = 4-16 channels, with BLASt3R's loss min_{α,β} |αF + β − log D*| (IRLS, stop-gradient through α, β) — [BLASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05210).
   - Data: GT depth from benchmark train splits (ScanNet++, HiRoom, Hypersim-like synthetic) or pseudo-depth from long PBA runs; depth supervision is far less data-hungry than pose because every pixel supervises.
   - Compute: head training ≤ 1 day on one 5090; at test time m extra parameters per frame in the LM system (m = 8 matches the current grid's size).
   - Cheapest first experiment: oracle fit — on dev, how much of DA3-SMALL's depth error (and recon F1) does the best linear combination of m channels recover vs the 8-cell grid, using simple hand-made channels (e.g. DA3 depth, its square, DA3 confidence-weighted edges) before training anything.
   - Risk: low-res DA3-SMALL features; channels may again absorb pose error (mitigate with the existing depth prior and a geometric row); novelty moderate (BLASt3R exists, but not for photometric BA on feed-forward outputs).

3. **Optimisation-aware heads via implicit differentiation through the PBA (Diff3R for camera BA), with learned per-frame prior weights.**
   - Hypothesis: training heads with the *post-PBA* pose loss through Λ(H+Λ)⁻¹ yields initialisations and per-frame trust weights that PBA converts into higher AUC@3 than the feed-forward-optimal head; learned Λ gets the benefit of pose priors on good frames without hurting frames needing large corrections.
   - Trained: (a) per-frame (6-DoF) prior-weight head on frozen DA3 tokens; (b) optionally LoRA on the camera head; gradients via one extra solve with the PBA's final damped normal matrix (already built analytically).
   - Data: needs accurate targets: GT train scenes or teacher (GIANT + long PBA); small heads limit data needs.
   - Compute: one extra linear solve per training sample on top of a 0.3 s PBA; DA3 forward with frozen backbone → thousands of samples per hour on a 5090.
   - Cheapest first experiment: head (a) only, trained on the benchmark train splits not in dev/held-out, compared against the globally tuned w_pose on dev and held-out; or, even cheaper, a non-learned per-frame Λ from DA3 confidence to check that per-frame trust helps at all.
   - Risk: IFT at non-converged LM points; small effect if PBA's solution is insensitive to priors; GT scarcity.

4. **TTO-ready feature head on frozen DA3/DéjàView tokens (GN-Net / PixLoc / Selfi for photometric BA).**
   - Hypothesis: an 8-channel head trained with a Gauss-Newton loss (or 3-5 unrolled PBA LM steps) widens the basin and sharpens the minimum relative to PCA-VGG, at lower cost (no VGG forward, less memory, fits 8 GB), and helps SMALL/BASE where VGG did not.
   - Trained: DPT-style head (~1-5M parameters) on DA3's 4 stored intermediate layers.
   - Data: correspondences from GT depth+poses (train splits) or from DA3 + long PBA pseudo-labels (self-supervised, unlimited; Selfi shows own-output targets work — [Selfi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08930)).
   - Compute: ≤ 1-2 days on one 5090 for the GN-loss variant; unrolled variant several times more.
   - Cheapest first experiment: GN-loss head on DA3-SMALL features trained on ScanNet++ train; drop into the `fmh` configuration in place of VGG; dev AUC@3/F1 and ms/iteration.
   - Risk: patch-14 token resolution may limit fine levels; the earlier raw-token result was neutral; gains may concentrate on F1 rather than pose.

5. **Dataset-scale self-distillation from model + PBA, including DéjàView loop reduction, judged at matched compute.**
   - Hypothesis: (a) DA3-SMALL fine-tuned on its own PBA outputs (LoRA3D at dataset scale, SelfEvo-style online EMA teacher with context asymmetry) becomes a better feed-forward predictor; (b) DéjàView K = 4/6 distilled from K = 12 (+ PBA) recovers much of K = 12's accuracy; the question is whether either beats "small model + PBA" at equal seconds.
   - Trained: LoRA on DA3-SMALL backbone (camera decoder frozen per SelfEvo's finding, or unfrozen to absorb PBA's camera corrections); for DéjàView, LoRA on the shared block with step conditioning for K = 4/6.
   - Data: unlabeled multi-view scenes; pseudo-labels from DA3 + PBA (~0.7 s per scene) or DéjàView K=12 (+PBA, ~1.3 s).
   - Compute: label generation ~1 h per several thousand scenes; fine-tuning in 6 h chunks; DéjàView fine-tuning is heavier (Solv3R used 1 week on 8 H100 — [Solv3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02857) — so restrict to LoRA and short schedules).
   - Cheapest first experiment: 500-2,000 unlabeled scenes, LoRA for DA3-SMALL at 24-48 views, evaluate SMALL alone and SMALL + PBA T = 25 on dev/held-out.
   - Risk (high): student inherits teacher's weak-direction errors so student + PBA ≈ teacher (see Q3 inference); prior runs showed view-count mismatch effects; DéjàView preprocessing sensitivity at low K.

6. **Failure probe → optimiser control (cheap, low ceiling).**
   - Hypothesis: a linear probe on DA3/DéjàView middle-layer tokens predicts per-frame gross failure and expected post-PBA error better than DA3 confidence (as for VGGT/π³: 93.5 % vs 79.7 % ROC-AUC — [probe paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35806)), and can gate relocalisation, choose among reference-view forward passes, or set per-frame priors.
   - Trained: logistic/linear regression on pooled tokens (minutes on CPU).
   - Data: benchmark train-split scenes with GT poses (labels = per-frame error after DA3 / after PBA).
   - Compute: negligible.
   - Cheapest first experiment: probe AUROC vs DA3 confidence (~0.90 in the knowledge base) on held-out ScanNet++/ETH3D frames.
   - Risk: the oracle fix of gross frames is worth only +1.5 AUC@3 / +4.1 AUC@30 on SMALL dev ([knowledge base](/rhome/pr26rb00/TUM-practical-compact-3d-distillation/knowledge/ideas/gross-failures-and-reference-view.md)); in the probe paper, filtering by the probe did not improve rotation averaging when failures were rare.

**How these combine with DéjàView's loop steps and DA3:** ideas 2-4 attach to frozen backbones and therefore work identically for DA3 sizes and DéjàView at any K (DéjàView decodes depth/ray maps through a head that can be extended); idea 1 needs only the PBA Hessian and a teacher; idea 5 is the only one requiring backbone/loop fine-tuning. For DéjàView specifically, Solv3R's evidence that intermediate loop states are step-conditioned argues for re-decoding at the chosen K and training heads per K rather than reusing K = 12 heads.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on (i) weak-subspace-targeted training, (ii) IFT training through camera/depth BA for pretrained multi-view transformers, (iii) matched-compute curves comparing output-space TTO, weight-space TTA and distillation; these are reasoned opportunities, not confirmed absences beyond the searches run (alphaXiv discovery on test-time training, self-distillation from BA, featuremetric learned features, differentiable BA, looped/iterative 3D transformers).
- Concurrent work risk is high in this area (multiple relevant papers appeared in Sep-Nov 2026: Self-Geometry, PURE, Solv3R, PosePlaner, BLASt3R, CSS-BA, the probe paper); a re-search shortly before submission is advisable.
- Compute estimates in the idea sketches are extrapolations from the project's measured per-scene timings, not measured training runs.
