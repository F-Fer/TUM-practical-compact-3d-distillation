\section{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Given a sequence of $N$ RGB images $(I_i)_{i=1}^N$, with
$I_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times W \times H}$, capturing the same 3D scene, the goal is to recover the camera parameters $\mathbf{g}_i = [\mathbf{q}_i, \mathbf{t}_i, \mathbf{f}_i] \in \mathbb{R}^9$, consisting of a rotation quaternion $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^4$, a translation $\mathbf{t}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and a field of view $\mathbf{f}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (principal point at the image center, first camera as the reference frame)~\cite{vggt}, and depth maps $D_i \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times H}$. The corresponding point maps $P_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times W \times H}$ are obtained by unprojecting the depth maps using the camera parameters.

A feed-forward model $f_\theta$ predicts these quantities:
\[
f_\theta : (I_i)_{i=1}^N
\mapsto (\mathbf{g}_i, D_i)_{i=1}^N.
\]
We consider a teacher $f_{\theta_T}$ (VGGT~\cite{vggt}, DéjàView~\cite{dejaview} or $\pi^3$~\cite{pi3}) and a smaller student $f_{\theta_S}$, with $|\theta_S| \ll |\theta_T|$. Predicting point maps through unprojection, rather than a dedicated prediction head, maintains consistency between the cameras and geometry during TTO~\cite{vggt}.

TTO refines the student's predictions over $T$ iterations using only the input images:
\[
(\hat{\mathbf{g}}_i,\hat{D}_i)_{i=1}^N
= \mathcal{O}_T\big(f_{\theta_S}((I_i)_{i=1}^N),
(I_i)_{i=1}^N\big).
\]

\begin{comment}
We measure the total computational cost as
\[
C = C_{\mathrm{fwd}}(\theta_S) + T\,C_{\mathrm{iter}},
\]
where $C_{\mathrm{fwd}}$ is the student's forward-pass cost and $C_{\mathrm{iter}}$ is the cost per optimization iteration, measured in FLOPs or wall-clock time. Our objective is to determine which student size and iteration count minimize reconstruction error under a fixed budget $C$, and how this trade-off compares with the teacher at the same budget.
\end{comment}

\subsection{Proposed Method}

\paragraph{Teacher and student models.}
We will select a teacher among VGGT~\cite{vggt}, DéjàView~\cite{dejaview}, and $\pi^3$~\cite{pi3}. Starting from the teacher architecture, we will train progressively smaller students, using OTT3R's public implementation as a starting point. Compression strategies include replacing the DINOv2 ViT-B encoder (86M parameters) with ViT-S (21M), reducing the number or width of transformer blocks. Another compression strategy would be to build a DéjàView-style model and train it to use fewer iterations $K$.

\paragraph{Distillation.}
Students will be trained to regress the teacher's depth and camera or ray predictions, weighted by the teacher's confidence. Following OTT3R~\cite{ott3r}, we will use strided view sampling to ensure that students learn camera poses as well as local geometry.

\paragraph{Test-time optimization.}
At inference, we will optimize camera parameters $\mathbf{g}_i$ and depth maps $D_i$ using multi-view consistency objectives. Candidate losses include reprojection error on correspondences and photometric or feature-metric consistency between views warped using the predicted depths and poses.

\subsection{Training}

We will select subsets of existing multi-view datasets with ground-truth geometry, such as CO3Dv2~\cite{co3dv2}, DL3DV~\cite{dl3dv}, and BlendedMVS~\cite{blendedmvs}. To broaden scene diversity, we may also use MegaDepth~\cite{megadepth}, ScanNet++~\cite{scannetpp}, Hypersim~\cite{hypersim}, or Virtual KITTI 2~\cite{virtualkitti2}.

Alternatively, following OTT3R~\cite{ott3r}, we will precompute teacher predictions as pseudo-labels. OTT3R uses 667K images across six datasets. Distillation allows the student to learn the teacher's predictions directly, while ground-truth supervision remains an alternative or complement.
