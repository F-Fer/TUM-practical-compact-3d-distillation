\section{Related Works}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Multi-View 3D Reconstruction}

Classical methods such as COLMAP~\cite{sfm} rely on feature matching and bundle adjustment. While being relatively accurate, they are slow and need a large amount of overlap between views and texture.

Learning-based approaches, including DUSt3R~\cite{dust3r} and MASt3R~\cite{mast3r}, predict dense geometry directly from images. VGGT~\cite{vggt} extends this approach to multiple views, while DéjàView~\cite{dejaview} and $\pi^3$~\cite{pi3} explore alternative architectures for efficient reconstruction and multi-view consistency.

\subsection{Reconstruction via Distillation}

Knowledge distillation compresses large models into smaller students while preserving their predictions~\cite{reconstructiondistillation,surveydistillation}.

OTT3R~\cite{ott3r} distills the 959M-parameter $\pi^3$ into a 102M-parameter student. However, its zero-shot performance degrades on out-of-distribution scenes, requiring costly domain-specific retraining to recover accuracy. Whether test-time optimization can mitigate this loss remains unexplored in this setting.

\subsection{Test-Time Optimization}

Test3R~\cite{test3r} improves reconstruction through cross-view consistency, while \emph{Learning 3D Reconstruction with Priors in Test Time}~\cite{reconstructionpriors} incorporates geometric priors during inference.

VGG-T$^3$~\cite{vggt3} uses test-time training to construct compact scene representations. However, these approaches do not directly investigate whether test-time optimization can compensate for the reduced capacity of a distilled reconstruction model.
